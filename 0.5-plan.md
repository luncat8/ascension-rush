# Plan 0.5 — Damage, Failures & Competitor AI

Subject scope: (a) damage and part-failure model — soft failures mid-flight
(engine out, tank leak, fairing loss) from low build strength, high-G, hard
landings, high-speed atmospheric entry, with repair costs; (b) a competitor
rocket company that also bids on contracts, builds rockets, flies them
(using the same autopilot), and takes market share — giving the player a
moving economy and occasional contract losses.

After 0.5 the game is feature-complete against the original draft; later
plans can add polish, progression, saves, or content.

Ships in two increments, each one measured before the next starts:

- **0.5.1 Damage & failures** — stress from the loads a flight carries, the
  failures that stress causes, hard-landing damage and the repair bill.
- **0.5.2 Competitor pressure** — a rival company bidding on the same board and
  moving the pad prices the player sells into.

### What a stage is rated for

A stage is rated against the world's own certified flight envelope — the
`maxThrustAcceleration` and `maxDynamicPressure` the autopilot flies — times a
margin (`structureMargin`, 1.35) times how strongly the stage was built (the
`strength` slider, 0.5…1). The reference build at 0.85 therefore flies the
certified envelope with room to spare, and a stage built to 0.5 is flown past
its rating by the same flight.

```
share   = load / limit                      # 0 … ∞, one per load channel
excess  = max(0, share - 1)                 # how far past the rating
stress += stressRate * (excessG² + excessQ² + fatigueWeight * (shareG² + shareQ²)) * dt
```

The fatigue term is what every hop costs, including one flown inside the
limits; the excess is what wrecks a stage. `load` is the non-gravitational
acceleration — thrust and drag, with gravity added back — because weight is not
a load a stage has to hold against itself. Stress is a fraction: 0 is new,
`ruptureStress` (1) is a tank that comes apart, `scrapStress` (0.75) is a stage
the turnaround replaces instead of repairing.

### What fails

```
rate = failureRate * stress^failureExponent + wearRate * wearExcess²
p    = 1 - exp(-rate * dt)
```

- `wearExcess` is how far an engine is past `wearStart` (0.8) of its rated burn
  time: an engine near its rating is the likeliest thing to fail on a stack
  that has been flying a while, and the overhaul the deck sells buys the
  seconds back.
- Sampling is exponential in `dt`, so the odds do not depend on the physics
  step or the time scale. The roll is seeded per flight
  (`damage.reset`, mulberry32), so the same flight fails the same way twice and
  a rate change can be measured against the run before it.
- Only the stage that is burning can fail, and only while it is burning.

A roll picks one mode, weighted: an engine that loses half its thrust
(`DEGRADED`, weight 3) is common, a dead engine (`ENGINE_OUT`, 1) is not, a
tank leak (`LEAK`, 2) is between, and a fairing only pops on a stack carrying
one (`FAIRING`, 1). Each mode changes the flight rather than annotating it: a
degraded engine loses half its `thrustMax` and some Isp, a dead one none of it
(and the autopilot stages on the spot), a leak grows from 0.8 kg/s and
accelerates, and a fairing loss puts the drag back and exposes the cargo.

A tank at `ruptureStress` with propellant aboard does not fail politely: the
stack comes apart (`mission.rupture`), the log records `cause: 'rupture'` and
the fleet instance is lost. Reaching it takes a flight far outside the
certified envelope — it is the manual-flight hazard, not an autopilot one.

### Touchdown

The 0.3.3 reference band — 2.7 to 3.8 m/s down, within a metre of the pad — is
the zero point of the landing scale, and the planet's crash limits are the far
end:

```
soft           = planet.landingVerticalSpeed * softLandingFraction   (0.5)
overVertical   = max(0, |vy| - soft)
overHorizontal = max(0, |vx| - soft * horizontalSpeed / verticalSpeed)
stress        += landingStress * (overVertical + 0.5 * overHorizontal)
payloadDamage += payloadLandingDamage * (…) * (fragile ? 2 : 1)
```

An autopilot landing therefore costs nothing and a hard one bills both the
stack and the cargo. The payout is for what arrives: `reward = quote *
(1 - payloadDamage)`, where the damage is the flight's own G load and the
arrival's hardness together. A fragile load above its own touchdown limit is
still lost outright, as 0.4.1 had it.

### Money

The repair is charged on the leg that flies the repaired stack, at the pad that
does the work, through `economy.repair` — the same shape as the 0.4.3
overhaul, quoted by `evaluateLeg` and therefore part of what the dispatch has
to afford. `parts.stageRepairValue` prices the stress a stage carries against
each part's `repairPerKg`, and a stage that came down with a dead engine buys a
whole new one. A stack is serviced before it launches: there is no choice to
fly damaged, so what the player decides is how strongly to build, which
profile to fly, and when to overhaul.

## Damage model — stress and failures

### Sources of stress
Track cumulative stress per part, per flight. Reset or reduce between
flights after repairs.
- **Launch G-stress**: integrate the magnitude of proper acceleration from
  non-gravitational loads (thrust and aerodynamic force divided by current
  mass) over time, applied to tanks and payload. Free-fall gravity is not a
  structural load in this approximation. Overbuilt rockets carry more mass;
  underbuilt parts use lower stress limits.
- **Aerodynamic stress (max-Q proxy)**: `0.5 * rho * v²` during ascent; if it
  exceeds a threshold dependent on tank strength and whether a fairing is
  present, accumulate stress. Without a fairing the payload also takes
  stress.
- **Engine burn wear**: `engineBurnTimeUsed` already exists (0.4); map to
  wear as `used / maxThrottleTime`; past 1.0 the engine has a rising chance
  of failure per second when throttled up.
- **Hard landing**: any touchdown with `|vy| > vSafe` (even non-crash) adds
  stress proportional to `(vy - vSafe)` to engines, tanks and payload;
  horizontal speed beyond `vSafeH` adds sideways stress. 0.3.3 records the
  measured touchdown velocities and the signed target-pad error in
  `game.lastReport`, and `experiments/logs/0.3.3-touchdowns.txt` measures the
  autopilot's reference arrivals: 2.7-3.8 m/s down and within 1 m of the pad on
  every route of all four worlds. Take that band as the zero-stress baseline and
  key damage on arrivals harder than it, not on guessed numbers.
- **Stage-separation shock**: small fixed stress per staging event (minor).

### Failure modes
For each stressed part, compute a failure rate per second that rises
non-linearly with stress:
`rate = clamp(k * (stress / stressLimit)^n, 0, maxRate)`.
For a sub-step of duration `dt`, sample with `p_step = 1 - exp(-rate * dt)`
(or an equivalent rate-correct method). This keeps failure odds independent
of the chosen physics sub-step. Use a seeded RNG in headless experiments so
rate changes can be compared reproducibly.
- **Engine failure (partial)**: current engine loses a fraction of thrust
  (e.g. 50 %) and its isp degrades; visible as flame sputter in HUD, red
  alarm. Autopilot detects and throttles up / stages early.
- **Engine failure (total)**: current engine drops to 0 thrust immediately;
  triggers automatic stage if next stage exists and has fuel (AP does
  this; manual player must press Space). If no next stage → coast / abort.
- **Tank leak**: current stage loses fuel at an accelerating rate (extra
  negative `dm/dt` added); no immediate thrust change but fuel runs out
  early.
- **Fairing loss** (if fairing fitted): stress pops it early (before
  intended altitude); drag jumps up and payload stress increases.
- **Payload damage**: fragile payloads under high G or hard landing lose
  a percentage of their reward (e.g. "80 % of cargo delivered, reward
  reduced accordingly"). Total loss if acceleration exceeds payload's
  fragility limit.
- **Catastrophic / RUD** (rapid unscheduled disassembly): if a tank's
  stress exceeds its rupture limit while it still contains fuel, the
  rocket explodes — immediate crash, no chance to save. Triggered by
  extreme max-Q (e.g. trying to fly at Mach 5 through thick lower
  atmosphere) or by hitting ground fast.

After a flight, surviving parts accumulate wear; repairs cost
`repair_cost = sum over parts (stress * part.mass * repairPerKg)` plus
engine overhaul if needed (from 0.4). Parts that suffered a total failure
or RUD are destroyed and must be rebought at full cost.

### Landing adjudication update (replaces 0.2 binary rule)
- `|vy| < vSafe && |vx| < vSafeH` and on target pad → perfect landing, no
  extra stress, full reward, small reliability bonus to the pilot/company.
- Soft-but-cant: within pad radius, slightly above vSafe but not crash
  (e.g. vy 8–15 m/s) → "rough landing", partial reward (depends on
  payload fragility), repair cost.
- Hard landing off pad: impact beyond the destruction threshold is a crash
  with total loss; reserve RUD for an in-flight structural/tank rupture.
- Missed pad but soft landing on terrain: "landed elsewhere", rocket
  survives but mission not completed; player can attempt a second leg or
  abandon (recovery fee?). Keep it simple: no on-ground travel, the
  mission is failed, rocket is recovered for a pad transport fee that
  scales with distance.

### Player feedback
- HUD shows part-stress bars (engine, tanks, payload) and aural/visual
  alarms on failures.
- Post-flight report: which parts were damaged, repair cost, final cash
  delta, cause of failure if any.

## Competitor AI
One competing company (name TBD, e.g. "Skybolt Logistics") operates in
parallel. It is not a physics simulation of *another* rocket flying at the
same time — the world only simulates one rocket at a time (performance +
clarity). Instead, every time the player lands (or fails) and the market
ticks, the competitor resolves one or more of its own flights using the
same autopilot/parts/economy rules, abstracted to fast simulation batches.

### What the competitor does
- Bids on the same contract board the player sees. Each contract has a
  probability of being taken by the competitor depending on its expected
  profit (from the competitor's builder + AP sim estimate) versus the
  player's cash position (the player getting rich makes the competitor
  more aggressive).
- Builds rockets from the same parts catalog with its own budget
  (`competitor.cash`). Its builds are not optimal — add a "skill" level
  that improves over time (e.g. it sometimes overbuilds, sometimes picks
  the wrong engine).
- Flies via autopilot. The fast simulation runs the same pure physics-step
  and guidance functions as player flight, with rendering/input omitted and
  reusable state buffers; avoid maintaining a second approximate integrator.
  It can crash and lose money, just like the player.
- Affects the market: with the current payload-mass contract model, track
  aggregate delivery volume per destination pad; heavy competitor service
  nudges that pad's `deliverPerKg` down, while underserved pads recover.
  If cargo classes are introduced later, split the pressure by class.
  Over time prices converge toward a dynamic equilibrium disturbed by
  random demand shocks (e.g. a pad suddenly pays 2× for food after a
  "storm").

### Scoreboard & progression
- HUD shows a small scoreboard: player cash, competitor cash, number of
  successful deliveries each, reputation.
- The competitor is *beatable* but not a pushover. Because it uses the
  same physics/economy, a skilled player (lighter builds, better routes,
  accepting a little extra risk via lower strength) out-earns it.
- No "win state" in 0.5; it's an open-ended sandbox. A future plan can add
  a campaign goal (e.g. monopolize 3 pads, first to $X, etc.).

### Failure model for the competitor
- It can crash too, at roughly the same rate the autopilot crashes given
  its build skill.
- It can also suffer part failures (since it shares the same damage
  roll). Badly-funded early-game competitor is more reckless and crashes
  more; as it earns cash it buys better parts, improves reliability,
  pressures the player.

## Tuning
- `experiments/failure.js` runs thousands of simulated flights with
  varying strength sliders and records: probability of engine-out,
  probability of RUD, expected repair cost per landing of a given
  hardness, expected lifetime of an engine vs. throttle duty cycle.
- Use the output to tune `k, n, stressLimit, repairPerKg` so that:
  - strength = 1.0 (overbuilt, heavy) → rare failures but lower margin
    (matches 0.4 tuning).
  - strength = 0.5 (skimpy build) → ~15–30 % chance of some failure per
    Verdant hop, with potentially large repair cost, but higher margin
    when successful.
  - "Good" balance at strength ~0.75: low failure rate, decent margin.
- Competitor aggressiveness: tune so on a fresh game it takes roughly
  30–40 % of available contracts; when the player is ahead it goes up to
  ~60 %; when behind it drops to ~20 %.

## Acceptance criteria for 0.5
- Failure modes trigger visibly in flight with HUD alarms; a tank leak
  causes fuel to deplete faster, a single-engine failure reduces or removes
  that stage's thrust, and RUD ends the mission with an explosion effect
  and report.
- Hard-but-not-crashing landings produce repair cost proportional to
  touchdown speed; soft landings produce none.
- Parts wear across flights as expected; engines demand overhaul after
  their rated burn time; worn parts fail at noticeably higher rates.
- The competitor appears in the scoreboard, takes contracts, delivers
  some and crashes some; over ~10 flights the player can see prices
  respond (deliverPerKg dropping at pads the competitor serves heavily).
- Running the game for a long session (10+ flights) does not leak memory
  or cause per-frame allocations to spike (verify with devtools memory
  profile); damage checks use preallocated scratch.
- All existing 0.1–0.4 acceptance criteria still hold (world rendering,
  flight physics, autopilot, market/profit, parts) — i.e. nothing
  regresses.
- The game is playable end-to-end against the original draft feature
  list: build a rocket, fly (manually or AP) between pads on different
  planets, make money, deal with wear/failures, compete against a rival.
