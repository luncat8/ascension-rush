# Plan 0.4 — Pad Markets, Contracts, Parts & Balance

Subject scope: make money the reason a leg is flown. The player should choose
*what* to deliver and *where*, price a build against a route instead of a
planet-wide constant, watch parts wear out, and be able to tell a well-flown
run from a wasteful one before it takes off.

Ships in four increments, each one measured before the next starts:

- **0.4.1 Pad markets & contract board** — per-pad prices that drift, a seeded
  contract board with urgency and fragile cargo, and a quoted payout per leg.
- **0.4.2 Parts catalog** — engines, tanks and fairings with mass, cost, Isp and
  service life; the builder becomes a bill of materials.
- **0.4.3 Service & wear** — engine burn time, overhaul gates, tank flights,
  and the `repairPerKg` hook 0.5 fills in.
- **0.4.4 Balance pass** — `experiments/balance.js` Monte Carlo, tuned
  constants, and the acceptance bands below.

## What 0.4 builds on (shipped and measured)

Everything here is already in the tree; the plan extends it instead of
re-deriving it.

- **The ledger** (`js/economy.js`): `spend`/`earn` are the only cash movements;
  a leg records `fuelCost` (fuel bought at load), `turnaroundCost` (structure
  replaced from the fleet instance's `restorePlan`), `structureCost` (capital at
  risk, recorded not charged), `autopilotFee` and `cashDelta`. A hand-flown leg
  pays no autopilot fee.
- **The scheduler seam** (`js/operations.js`): `payloadProvider(mass, limit)` is
  the single place that decides whether a payload is available, called from
  `evaluateLeg`, `routeReadiness`, `dispatch`, `changeRouteType` and
  `saveRoute`. 0.4.1 replaces this one function with the market; nothing else in
  the scheduler moves.
- **Per-leg quotes**: `operations.evaluateLeg` already returns
  `{ ready, reason, rocket, cost, fuelMass, dryMass, twr }` and the deck quotes
  it. The market adds `pricePerKg` and `reward` to that record.
- **Planet prices** are `planet.prices = { fuel, steel, delivery }` and the
  flight-log rows carry `revenue` per leg, so route profitability is aggregation
  rather than new instrumentation.
- **Reference flights** (`experiments/logs/0.3.5-touchdowns.txt`, 12/12 routes
  on all four worlds in all three profiles) are the physics baseline. **No
  increment in 0.4 may move them.** If a planned change cannot preserve an
  entry, re-measure, record a new log, and say in the worklog which number moved
  and why.

## 0.4.1 Pad markets & contract board

### Market state

One market per run, reset by `operations.initialize` (the whole per-world
reset), so no price crosses a planet switch.

```
R.market = {
	state: {
		planetId, seed, turn,
		pads: [ { padId, fuel, steel, delivery, served } ],   // live prices, money per kg
		contracts: [],                                        // open and assigned
		nextContractId
	}
}
```

- Each pad starts at `planet.prices` and drifts on every dispatch turn:
  `value += (base − value) * meanReversion + (rand * 2 − 1) * drift * base`,
  clamped to `base * (1 ± spread)`. Mean reversion keeps prices from sticking to
  a bound after a delivery pushes one down; the random walk gives a pad a cheap
  week worth waiting for.
- Prices are seeded: `market.initialize(seed)` with `seedFor(planetId)`, so a
  world reproduces the same market and tests can assert exact values. One RNG
  (mulberry32, `js/market.js`) is advanced only at generation and turn time —
  never in a frame.
- Serving a destination (`market.fulfil`) nudges that pad's `delivery` price
  down by `servedPressure` (per delivery) and increments `served`. This is the
  hook 0.5's competitor pressure plugs into; in 0.4.1 the player's own volume is
  the only pressure.

### Contracts

```
contract = {
	id, fromPadId, toPadId,
	payloadMass,        // drawn from the planet's payload ladder
	perKg,              // fixed at posting: destination price × (urgency + fragile) premium
	turnsLeft,          // whole dispatch turns, not wall clock
	fragile,            // stricter touchdown limit, cargo lost if it is exceeded
	status,             // 'open' | 'assigned'
	postedTurn
}
```

- The board keeps `contractSlots` open contracts per pad. A turn advances when a
  leg ends (delivered, landed, crashed, return-blocked): `market.advance()` drifts
  prices, ages contracts, drops the expired ones and refills each board.
- The payload ladder is `[0.5, 1, 1.5] × planet.defaultPayload`, clamped to
  `10 … constants.rocket.maxPayloadMass` and rounded to 10 kg, so a board asks
  for the reference build and for one that needs a bigger type.
- Fragile cargo is delivered only if the touchdown is under
  `planet.landingVerticalSpeed * fragileSpeedFactor`; otherwise the cargo is
  lost (`status: landed`, `cargoLost` in the log) and the contract goes back on
  the board with its remaining turns.
- Taking a contract reserves it (`assign`); delivering fulfils it (`fulfil`,
  removed, `served` counted); any unsuccessful end of the leg that carried it
  releases it (`release`, back to `open`). A contract can therefore be retried
  with a gentler profile, but not after its turns run out.

### Pricing a leg

`market.quote(fromPadId, toPadId, payloadMass, contract)` is the one reward
function, called once per leg at dispatch and frozen on the flight record:

```
contract leg:  reward = payloadMass * contract.perKg
standing leg:  reward = payloadMass * price(destination).delivery
                        * (1 + distanceBonus * distance / circumference)
```

- A standing service (any dispatch or route without a contract) sells at the
  destination's live price; the board is a premium market with urgency and
  fragile cargo on top.
- Quoting at dispatch, not at touchdown, means an in-flight price drift cannot
  change what the player was promised, and the deck, the log and the debrief all
  quote the same number.
- Fuel and steel are bought at the departure pad's live price, so tanking up at
  a cheap pad is real money. `economy.priceFuel(padId)` / `priceSteel(padId)` /
  `priceDelivery(padId)` replace the planet-wide getters; every call site passes
  the pad it is standing on.

## 0.4.2 Parts catalog

`js/parts.js` (data + lookups) and `constants.parts` (the catalog tables, all
named constants): parametric parts, no art.

```
engine  { id, label, thrustPerFuelMass, ispSea, ispVac, baseMass, thrustToMass, costPerMass, maxThrottleSeconds, reliability }
tank    { id, label, massPerFuelMass, costPerMass }
fairing { id, label, mass, costPerMass, dragFraction }   // top stage only
```

- **Strength is a per-stage slider, not a tank field.** A tank part defines only
  its weight efficiency `massPerFuelMass` and `costPerMass`; the *built* strength
  (0.5–1) is the stage's own slider, feeding the structure factor
  `0.65 + 0.35 * strength` exactly as 0.3 did. This keeps 0.5's stress story
  attached to the stage, not to a tank preset, and means a "light tank" reads as
  a lower `massPerFuelMass` while the player still dials its strength.
- **Continuity is the design rule.** The 0.3 model is the catalog's first entry:
  `thrust = fuelMass * thrustPerFuelMass` (72 N per kg),
  `dryMass = fuelMass * massPerFuelMass * (0.65 + 0.35 * strength) + baseMass +
  thrust / thrustToMass` (0.075, 60 kg, 150 N/kg), Isp 265/330 s. The default
  engine and tank in `constants.parts` carry those exact numbers, so a reference
  build weighs and flies exactly what it weighs and flies today; the envelope
  tables are the proof. The `strength` field on the tank in the earlier draft was
  dropped — it is the stage slider's job.
- A stage becomes `{ fuelMass, engineId, tankId, fairingId | null, strength }`.
  Missing ids are filled by `parts.sanitizeStage` (default engine + default tank,
  no fairing), so types built before the catalog still load without a migration
  step and any leftover `{ fuelMass, strength }` config resolves to defaults.
  The type snapshot and the flight log keep the ids; `rocket.stageDryMass`,
  `rocket.stageThrust` and `rocket.applyFleetState` (which now reads Isp and
  thrust from the engine) read the parts. `restorePlan` / `economy.turnaround`
  price a turnaround from the parts the rebuilt stages carry.
- **Mass and cost, one place.** `parts.stageBreakdown(stage)` returns
  `{ engineMass, tankMass, fairingMass, dryMass, thrust }`:
  `tankMass = fuel * tank.massPerFuelMass * (0.65 + 0.35 * strength)`,
  `engineMass = engine.baseMass + thrust / engine.thrustToMass`,
  `fairingMass = fairing ? fairing.mass : 0` (top stage only). Structure cost is
  `(Σ engineMass * engine.costPerMass + Σ tankMass * tank.costPerMass +
  fairingMass * fairing.costPerMass) * priceSteel(pad)`, and for the default
  parts `costPerMass` is 1 everywhere, so it equals `dryMass * priceSteel` and the
  0.4.1 money baseline is untouched. The builder's cost panel breaks out engines,
  tanks, fairing and fuel from `parts.typeStructureMass` / `parts.typeBuildCost`.
- The player's choices become real through *alternatives*, not by editing the
  default: a high-thrust booster (more N per kg, heavier, thirstier, pricier),
  a vacuum engine (better Isp, less thrust), light tanks (lower `massPerFuelMass`,
  pricier, more 0.5 failures) and reinforced tanks (heavier, cheaper, longer
  life in 0.4.3). Every alternative is data; none changes the formula.
- Builder UI: engine / tank / fairing pickers per stage beside the fuel and
  strength sliders (fairing only enabled on the top stage); the cost panel breaks
  out engines, tanks, fairing and fuel. Parts are chosen, priced and shown in the
  builder and in the dispatch card.
- **Fairings** reduce the drag reference area while attached and add mass. The
  whole-rocket `referenceArea` is passed into `aerodynamics.calculate` as an
  optional argument; when a fairing is attached the effective area is
  `referenceArea * fairing.dragFraction`. It is jettisoned at
  `constants.parts.fairingAltitude` (mass shed from the top stage's `dryMass`,
  drag returns to full). Default builds select no fairing, so the envelope is
  unchanged; a fairing is opt-in and its `fairingCost` is charged once at build,
  never per leg.

## 0.4.3 Service & wear

- `engineBurnTimeUsed` accumulates on the fleet instance (seconds at > 0
  throttle); `maxThrottleSeconds` blocks a launch with `NEEDS OVERHAUL` until
  `engine.cost * overhaulFactor` is paid on the leg that buys it,
  `overhaulCost` in the log.
- Engines and tanks carry `maxFlights` / `lifeFlights`; a tank past its rating
  must be replaced at turnaround price, which is what `structureCost` tracking
  already measures.
- `repairPerKg` and `stress` fields exist but stay 0: 0.5 fills them from
  touchdown hardness. `repairCost` is quoted in the log from 0.4.3 on, charged
  from 0.5.

## 0.4.4 Balance pass

- `experiments/balance.js` (node, seeded) flies `operations.dispatch` through
  the real autopilot and physics, for each world × each distance bracket ×
  each stage count × payload %, and reports a profit-margin histogram plus the
  cash distribution per bucket. It reads prices from `market` with the
  configured seed.
- The pre-wear numbers this has to move onto the target bands, measured on
  0.4.1: a Verdant reference hop flown back and forth on refuelled turnarounds
  pays **~80 %** margin, an out-and-back that needs a stage rebuilt pays
  **~66 %**, and the first leg of a run (a granted, fully fuelled rocket) pays
  **~90 %**. Delivery prices are still the 0.3 planet defaults while the only
  per-flight costs are fuel and replaced structure, so most of 0.4.4's work is
  on the revenue side — and it must run *after* 0.4.3, whose overhaul and tank
  life are the per-flight costs the bands are meant to include.
- Target bands on Verdant's shortest hop, balanced profile, reference build:
  - correctly sized build, standing service: **+10 … +20 %** margin;
  - 3-stage overbuild on the same hop: **≤ 0 %**;
  - crash: cost of the lost stack (a large negative), never a mid-band result;
  - contract with urgency or fragile cargo: higher margin, higher variance;
  - a route flown twice with the first leg's leftover fuel: strictly better
    margin than a refuelled turnaround, so `fuelPolicy: none` has a reason.
- Tune `constants.market` (spread, drift, bonuses, ladder), `constants.parts`
  cost fields and `constants.economy.startingCash`; keep them named constants,
  no magic numbers inline.
- Record the run in `experiments/logs/0.4.4-balance.txt` and quote the bands it
  actually produced.

## Acceptance criteria for 0.4

- A properly sized reference build on a Verdant short hop is profitable
  (≈10–20 % margin) as a standing service, and the deck shows that margin before
  dispatch; an overbuilt stack shows ≤ 0 %.
- Pad prices differ and drift between flights, at least one arbitrage route
  exists (cheap fuel on one pad, a rich destination on another), and the flight
  log can prove it: `fuelCost + turnaroundCost` versus `revenue` per leg.
- Contracts are quotable, takeable, refusable, expirable and lost: the log
  records the contract, the price per kg, and `cargoLost` when fragile cargo
  breaks.
- Parts are chosen, priced and worn: an engine past its rated burn time blocks
  launch with `NEEDS OVERHAUL` until paid; a type's parts are visible in its
  snapshot and in every flight-log row.
- `experiments/balance.js` runs under node, is seeded, and prints the margin
  summary that matches the target bands.
- No per-frame allocations from market, parts or wear code: all of it runs at
  build, dispatch, touchdown and turn boundaries.
- Every existing 0.1–0.3 acceptance criterion still holds: the same 12/12
  reference routes on every world and profile, the same staged-rocket physics,
  the same operations reasons, and the same 585+ assertions green.
