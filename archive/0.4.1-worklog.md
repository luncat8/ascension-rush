# Worklog — 0.4.1 Pad markets & contract board

## Plan review
`0.4-plan.md` was rewritten before this increment, because as written it did not
describe this codebase:

- It listed `js/hud.js`, which does not exist (the flight HUD is `js/render.js`
  and the operations UI is `js/deck.js`), and put the market and parts UI in a
  file nobody loads.
- Its cost model contradicted the ledger 0.3.5 shipped: it charged fuel per kg
  burned (the ledger buys fuel at load time and only meters the burn),
  and priced parts as a new cash cost on every flight (0.3.5 prices the
  structure a turnaround replaces, and records `structureCost` as capital at
  risk, not a charge).
- It had no ordering: parts, market, wear and balance were one lump. It is now
  four increments, each with its own validation, and a continuity rule: **no
  0.4 change may move a 0.3.5 reference touchdown entry**; the envelope tables
  are the proof, and a change that cannot preserve them must re-measure and
  record which number moved.
- It used constants that do not exist (`PRICE_ENGINE_T40`) and left the reward
  formula half-specified. 0.4.1's numbers are now `constants.market`, and the
  quote formula is one function.

One design change against the plan: the market is **seeded per world**
(`market.seedFor(planetId)`), not randomly walked from a hidden state. A world
reproduces its prices and its opening board, which is what made the whole
increment testable with exact numbers, and it costs the player nothing (the
drift between flights still moves every price).

## Implemented
- **`js/market.js`** (new, 265 lines). Per-pad `fuel`/`steel`/`delivery` prices
  starting at the planet's list price and drifting one bounded step per turn
  (`meanReversion` pulls them back, `padSpread` bounds them), a `served` count
  per pad, and a contract board of `constants.market.contractSlots` (3) open
  contracts per pad. `quote(from, to, payload, contractId)` is the single
  reward function: a contract pays its posted `perKg`, a standing service pays
  the destination's live price plus `distanceBonus * distance / circumference`.
  Determinism is one mulberry32 seeded from `seedFor(planetId)`, advanced only
  at generation and turn time.
- **Contracts** are posted from the planet's payload ladder with a deadline of
  2–4 turns, a fragility flag and a rate that grows as the deadline shortens.
  `assign` takes one off the board (its clock stops while it flies); `fulfil`
  removes it, counts the delivery on the destination and nudges that pad's
  delivery price down; `release` puts it back with its turns left when the leg
  that carried it does not deliver. A fragile contract is lost if the touchdown
  is above `planet.landingVerticalSpeed * fragileSpeedFactor`, and the log
  records `cargoLost`.
- **One market turn is one finished leg** (`operations.applyLegResult`), so
  urgency counts dispatches, not wall clock, and it advances for crashes and
  blocked returns as well as deliveries.
- **`js/economy.js`** now prices at the pad: `priceFuel(padId)`,
  `priceSteel(padId)`, `priceDelivery(padId)`, and `refuel`/`turnaround`/
  `buyRocket`/`estimateBuildCost` take the pad the money is spent at. No other
  cash rule changed.
- **`js/mission.js`** pays the frozen quote: `operations.beginLeg` asks the
  market once and puts `rewardQuote`, `rewardPerKg`, `fragileSpeed` and
  `contractId` on the flight record. A price that drifts mid-flight cannot
  change what the deck promised.
- **`js/deck.js`** gains the Market tab (per-pad price table, `served`, and the
  board with a "Load into dispatch" button per contract), a contract card on
  the dispatch view (posted rate, payout, turns left, fragile limit, clear
  button), the quoted payout in the dispatch summary, and `$/kg` plus cargo
  lost in the flight log. The outbound payload slider is pinned while a
  contract is loaded; changing pads or payload drops the contract rather than
  silently flying someone else's cargo with the wrong freight.
- **`index.html` / `css/style.css`**: the Market tab, the contract card and the
  contract rows live in the shipped markup so `experiments/dom.js` keeps it
  honest.

## Validation
- **`experiments/market-test.js`** (new, 89 assertions): a run reproduces its
  seeded prices and board and another seed does not; every price stays inside
  the pad spread for 200 turns; a price pushed to a bound recovers; pads really
  do quote different fuel; boards are full and every contract is well formed
  (other pad, payload inside the catalog bounds and rounded, turns in range,
  premium over the standing price); the quote formula for standing services and
  for contracts; the quote is frozen at dispatch across two turns of drift; the
  whole contract life — assigned, taken twice, mismatched payload, mismatched
  pad, unknown id, delivered (with the pad's price pushed down and the board
  refilled), released on a wrong-pad landing, released on a crash, expired by
  turns, and not aged while it is flying; fragile cargo destroyed on a hard
  touchdown and paid on a soft one; a real autopilot flight on a real contract.
- Updated, not weakened: `flight-test` pays the dispatch quote and checks the
  distance factor, `operations-test` reads prices at the pad that spent the
  money, `render-smoke-test` drives the shipped markup through the Market tab,
  loading a contract into the dispatch card and clearing it again.
- All 9 suites pass: 684 assertions (was 585; market 89, render-smoke +9).

## Measured, for 0.4.4
The market makes the margin visible, and it is still far too easy: on Verdant a
reference hop flown back and forth on refuelled turnarounds pays **~80 %**
margin, an out-and-back that needs a stage rebuilt pays **~66 %**, and a run's
first leg (a granted, fully fuelled rocket) pays **~90 %**. Delivery prices are
still the 0.3 planet defaults while the only per-flight costs are fuel and
replaced structure, so most of the tuning is on the revenue side — and it has
to wait for 0.4.3, because engine overhaul and tank life are the per-flight
costs the target bands are supposed to include.

## Bugs the tests caught
- `market.advance()` aged and expired the contract a leg was *carrying* before
  the leg settled it. Assigned contracts now keep their clock, and a released
  contract comes back with at least one turn.
- The dispatch summary quoted fuel and structure at the planet's list price
  while the ledger charged the pad's, so the card could promise a cheaper leg
  than the one that flew.
