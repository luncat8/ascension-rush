# Worklog — 0.4.3 Service & wear

## Starting point

0.4.2 plus its review pass: parts are measured and priced through one structure
value, the envelope is byte-identical to `experiments/logs/0.3.5-touchdowns.txt`
and all 10 suites pass. The seam this increment hooks into is the fleet
instance's `stageState`, `rocket.restorePlan`, `operations.planCost`/`evaluateLeg`
and `parts.stageStructureValue`.

## Implemented

### Wear lives on the fleet instance

A stage slot now carries `engineBurnTimeUsed`, `lifeFlights` and `stress` next to
`alive`/`fuelMass` (`rocket.createStageState` → `createStageSlot`), and the flight
stage carries the same three (`rocket.create`), because the seconds are counted in
the physics step and have to survive the landing: `applyFleetState` lends them to
the stack, `captureStageState` takes them back, and `copyStageState` moves them
onto the instance. `rocket.resetStageWear` clears all three, and a stage the
turnaround replaces is a new stage — so a rebuilt booster has a fresh engine and
tank while a stage that only refuelled keeps what is on it.

- **Seconds at throttle**: `physics.step` adds `dt` to the burning stage inside the
  existing burn branch. A number, not an allocation, so the frame loop is
  unchanged in that respect.
- **Legs flown**: `operations.applyLegResult` increments `lifeFlights` on every
  stage that came back attached. A stage left in the air is replaced and starts
  over.

### Engines: `NEEDS OVERHAUL`

`engine.maxThrottleSeconds` is now finite for every engine (900 standard, 600
booster, 1200 vacuum) and `constants.parts.overhaulFactor` is 0.35.
`parts.engineOverdue` / `burnTimeLeft` / `stageOverhaulValue` / `overhaulValue`
price the service: `engineMass * costPerMass * overhaulFactor` in steel-kg, summed
over the *attached* engines that are overdue (a stage already being rebuilt is not
serviced twice).

The gate is explicit, on the leg: `evaluateLeg(..., overhaul)` refuses with
`NEEDS OVERHAUL` and quotes `overhaulValue`/`overhaulCost`; an authorized leg
spends `economy.overhaul`, records `overhaulCost` on the flight and in the log
row, and zeroes the seconds. The dispatch card shows a service row
(`S1 45/900 s · 0/12 legs`), the price when one is due, and one button that
authorizes this leg. A **saved route is always authorized** — a standing service
budgets its own maintenance rather than stalling on the pad; a hand-sent leg asks
the player. The authorization is cleared when the rocket, the type or the pad
changes, and after the send.

### Tanks: `maxFlights`

`tank.maxFlights` (12 standard, 6 light, 24 reinforced) against the slot's
`lifeFlights`. `parts.wornOut` makes a flown-out tank part of the next turnaround:
`rocket.restorePlan` prices that stage's structure and a full fuel load even
though the stage landed, and `fullStageState` rebuilds it with fresh wear. A
no-refuel leg still buys nothing and flies the worn tank — which is what
`fuelPolicy: none` is for.

### Repair, quoted not charged

Every engine, tank and fairing carries `repairPerKg` (0) and every slot carries
`stress` (0). `parts.stageRepairValue` / `repairValue` compute the bill 0.5 will
charge; `repairCost` is recorded on the flight and in every log row now, and is
always 0 until 0.5 fills `stress` from touchdown hardness.

### Money and UI

`economy.overhaul` is a spend like fuel and turnaround, and `economy.beginFlight`
itemizes `overhaulCost` in `cashDelta`. The deck's leg quote reads
`fuel · structure · overhaul`, the log card adds the overhaul when a leg paid one,
and the maintenance row reuses the existing `offer-card`/`ghost-button` styles (no
new CSS).

## Validation

- **New `experiments/service-test.js`, 68 `assert` calls** (11 suites now): the
  catalog is rated and finite; a real Verdant hop leaves **44.9 s** on the engine
  that burned and one leg on every stage that came back; the flight state and the
  instance agree on the seconds; a turnaround hands back a new stage and keeps the
  wear on a refuelled one; a light tank at its rating is priced and replaced by the
  next turnaround and by nothing on a no-refuel leg; an overdue engine refuses the
  leg with `NEEDS OVERHAUL`, quotes **91.56 steel-kg ≈ $229**, and the authorized
  leg spends exactly that through the ledger, records it in the log and zeroes the
  seconds; the cash paid equals what the leg itemizes; a route services itself;
  `repairPerKg`/`stress` still cost nothing. The gate assertion was checked to fail
  with the gate removed.
- **`render-smoke-test`** covers the card end to end: the clock, the blocked
  reason, the price, the authorize button releasing the send button, and the
  overhaul itemized on the leg. Writing that test caught a real bug in the new
  code — `renderDispatch` still called `evaluateLeg` without `form.overhaul`, so
  the button set the flag and the card kept refusing. The card now evaluates with
  the authorization it is displaying.
- **Flight did not move.** All 48 reference route rows from
  `experiments/envelope.js <world> 0 balanced` are byte-identical to
  `experiments/logs/0.3.5-touchdowns.txt`, and `build-sweep 60 1` is still
  218/226/229/230 with **0 regressions**. Wear is money and a gate; the harness
  flies one leg per fresh instance, so no reference flight can reach a rating.
- All 11 suites pass (852 `assert` call sites).

## What the numbers say for 0.4.4

Measured on Verdant's shortest hop, Homeport → Eastport, reference build, turn-0
prices (steel $2.496/kg, fuel $1.415/kg):

- revenue for 80 kg ≈ **$5 324**; fuel for the leg ≈ **$520**; an overhaul
  ≈ **$229** every ~20 legs (44.9 s per leg against 900 s) ≈ **$11 per leg**.
- So wear as tuned is worth ~2 % of a leg's revenue, and a tank's life limit only
  bites on a build that reuses a stage 6–12 times. That is deliberately gentle —
  it makes the mechanic visible without moving the 0.3 flight baseline — but it
  means **0.4.4's margin bands will not be reached by wear alone**: the revenue
  side (delivery prices, contract premia) is still where the 80–90 % margins come
  from, and `overhaulFactor` / the ratings / `maxFlights` are the knobs if wear is
  meant to carry more of the cost.
