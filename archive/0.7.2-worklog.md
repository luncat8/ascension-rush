# Worklog 0.7.2 — The chart's pole leaves with the ship

## Starting point

0.7.1 shipped the deck and HUD. The chart itself — `js/coords.js` + `js/sky.js`
+ `js/render.js`, the 0.3.7/0.3.8 work — was one log-polar map of the plane
with a single shared scale on both axes, seated on one **pivot** body whose
centre was the log **pole**. Three things about that were wrong, and they were
visible in `experiments/body-hop.html`:

1. The rocket was drawn on the same lane as a moon while its speed did not
   match: the ship and the bodies were not charted by the same rule.
2. Flying onto a body did nothing to the projection. The body stayed a lens
   the hull stopped in front of; its surface was never unwrapped.
3. Flying out of the world left the world's centre as the pole, so the chart
   never became the ship's own.

All 21 test/sweep files and the stored balance log were green before the first
edit, and are green after.

## What changed

**The pole travels with the ship (`js/sky.js`, `solveTarget`).** The pole is
now a point on the ray from the pivot's centre to the ship. On the ground it is
the centre, so the pivot's surface is the flat ground line the ladder starts at.
Above the air it travels out along that ray and **stops on the pivot's surface
under the ship** — a full body diameter out, `view.orbit`, one eased number
covering both ways. The ladder start is the pole (`pose.ladderRef =
pivot.radius − poleDistance`) and the target distance is the hull's altitude
above it (`targetRadius − poleDistance`), so the hull holds the origin line and
the ground line sits exactly the hull's altitude below it.

Stopping on the surface rather than going out to the hull is the whole fix. The
chart's height is distance *from the pole*; a pole on the hull puts every
bearing at the same range at the same height, so the world it left is charted
*above* the ship. Measured before the correction: the world's lens centre sat at
`y = 141` while the hull was at `y = 496`. With the surface pole the world curve
tops out at `y = 922` against a hull at `y = 496` and a ground line at `y = 1887`
— the world is below the hull, which is the only way round that reads.

**Approach and departure are automatic (`js/sky.js`).** `sky.approachIndex`
claims the body whose centre the hull is inside — by distance only, never by
where it is on screen — and `approachSeat` seats on it through the existing
hand-over, so flying into a body unwraps it with no click. `pivot.approach`
marks the seat; leaving the body releases it, so a seat the player asked for
stays put and a seat the flight made is handed back. The two reaches are the
hysteresis: `constants.sky.approachRadii = 6`, `departRadii = 10`.

**One curve for the ground (`js/render.js`, `solveGround`).** The ground curve
is now solved for *every* pole position: flat at the centre, bending into a
horizon as the pole travels, that horizon once it stands on the surface. The
interior, the cutaway bands and the air column are drawn only while the pole is
inside (`curves.radial`); from the surface there is something to stand on and
nothing behind it. `curves.ground = -2` remains, but only for a pole a gesture
has walked past the sphere, where no ray exits it forward.

**`sky.shipPlane` uses `R.camera.worldRadius`,** the radius the projection
itself uses, so the pole that stands on the surface under the hull and the hull
the chart draws are the same point.

**`sky.enclosingDistance` clamps its root at zero.** A ray that leaves the pole
grazing the sphere exits at the pole itself, the root rounds a hair below zero
there, and a negative distance is the "no exit" sentinel — not a place on the
chart. This was a live defect, not a hardening: a 0→30 km climb sweep had 1452
frames with `NaN` in `curves.groundYs`, feeding 34 222 `NaN` `lineTo` calls
through `traceCurve` ← `drawGround`. It is 0 now.

**`sky.ease` snaps shut.** The ladder is a log, so the last part-per-million of
an eased distance is still tenths of a nat on screen; without the snap the pole
asymptotes toward its goal and the hull asymptotes toward its own line forever.

**`experiments/body-hop.html`** — the probe is draggable on both axes
(sideways = longitude, up = the ladder with auto-climb at the top edge), and the
readout now names the pivot, whether the seat was flown into or asked for, how
far the pole is out of the pivot and whether its surface curves, the target's
distance from the pole, the ladder start and the ground line.

**`flight-sky-plan.md` §0.3.7** — the design artifact now states the pole law
("The pole leaves with the ship"), the automatic hand-over, and the ground as
one continuous curve; the ground bullet's "outside the ground sphere there is no
curve at all" regime is gone.

`shipY == originY` above the air and `groundY − originY` exactly the climb, with
no frame of the climb moving the hull more than a few pixels.

The lab page, driven by synthetic pointer events through its own inline script
and `js/input.js` (no browser):

- Drag the probe up out of the air: the pole goes out, the ladder start follows
  it, `ground` stays the world's curve.
- Fly it onto Lune (56 pointer moves): `pivot 3 (Lune) approach true`, pole at
  Lune's centre, ladder start 2.10 R, Lune's own lens gone, its surface the flat
  ground line, hull above it.
- Fly it clear and out to 120 R (20 pointer moves): `pivot −1 approach false`,
  pole 1.000 pivot radii out, ladder start 0, `ground −1` — the world back as
  the curving ground under the hull.
- 0 `NaN` draw calls in every phase.

`experiments/chart-test.js` gained the same three legs as assertions: pole and
ladder start at both ends, the hull on the origin line with the ground exactly
the climb below it, no frame of a flown climb jumping the hull, each body's lens
centre equal to its own plane point through `camera.projectPlane` (and the hull
the same map), the approach seat unwrapping Lune into flat ground, and the
departure handing the chart back. Three mutations confirm the assertions bind:
pinning the pole to the centre, disabling `approachSeat`, and reading the target
from the centre each fail a different assertion.

All 21 `experiments/*-test.js` and sweep files pass; `node experiments/balance.js
20261004 4 2` is byte-identical to `experiments/logs/0.5.2-balance-pressure.txt`.

## Also verified in a browser

`experiments/body-hop.html` in headless Chromium (puppeteer-core +
`@sparticuz/chromium`, tooling in the scratch dir, not the repo), driven with
real pointer events and screenshotted at each stage (`shots/` in the
workspace): landed in the air with the pole at Verdant's centre and its surface
the flat ground line; dragged above the air, where the hull holds the origin
line, Verdant's surface curves away below it and Helios, Ember, Pale and Lune
are lenses charted from the pole; and placed two Lune radii above Lune, where
the approach rule — no button — seats the chart (`pivot 3, approach true`,
ground Lune), the scale relabels itself "DISTANCE FROM LUNE", Verdant is a
lens, and Lune's surface is the flat ground strip under the probe.

## Not verified

The drag feel and the readout's readability at real DPI are still unchecked —
the browser was headless and the pointer synthetic. The 400 km row is a
diagnostic teleport, not a flown ascent; the flown check is the 4700-frame
climb in the chart test, which stops at 30 km.

## Next step

Fly it in a browser and settle the two things the stub cannot show: how the
ground curve's horizon reads while the pole is travelling (the one part of the
law nobody has seen drawn), and whether `approachRadii` of 6 is the right reach
for the two planets — at 6 a hull crossing Vesper's orbit gets pulled onto it
long before it looks close. If the reach needs to be per-body, that is a
constant on the catalog entry, not a second rule.
