# Worklog — 0.4.4 Balance pass

## Starting point

0.4.3 plus its review pass. The physics baseline is fixed and measured: 48
reference route rows byte-identical to `experiments/logs/0.3.5-touchdowns.txt`,
`build-sweep 60 1` at 218/226/229/230 with 0 regressions, 867 assertions green.
Delivery prices were still the 0.3 planet defaults while the only per-flight
costs were fuel and replaced structure, so the plan's pre-wear numbers stood at
~80 % for a refuelled turnaround and ~90 % for a granted first leg.

## `experiments/balance.js`

A seeded Monte Carlo that flies the real thing: `operations.dispatch` through the
real autopilot and physics, one bucket per world × distance bracket × stage count
× payload rung, `legs` legs per repetition, each bucket's seed mixed from the
base seed so one command line reproduces a whole report.

- **What it measures**: the flight log row of every leg — status, `revenue`,
  `cashDelta`, the pad it landed on. A delivered leg's margin is
  `cashDelta / reward`: the payout less the 10 % autopilot fee, the fuel it
  refuelled, the structure the turnaround replaced and the overhaul it bought.
- **The granted opener is counted apart**. A chain opens on the granted, fully
  fuelled rocket, whose first leg buys no fuel and books ~+90 %. It is the run's
  bonus, not the service's steady state, so it is tallied in a `grant` column and
  left out of the margin histogram. Without that the mean of a 4-leg chain sits
  ~9 points above the service it is supposed to measure.
- **Reports**: the per-world histogram table (margin, p10, p90, cash min/end,
  and why a chain stopped), then the five bands from the plan.

Three bugs found while building it, all worth recording: the flight log is
newest-first (`entries[0]` is the leg that just finished, `entries[length - 1]`
is the oldest — reading the wrong end made every leg look like the granted
opener and dispatched leg 2 from the wrong pad); a world's buckets have to run
after `R.world.initialize(world.id)` or they read the previous world's pads and
prices; and `harness.createGame` ignored a cash argument, which the reports need.

## The tuning

The margin of a leg is `0.9 − cost / reward`, and for the reference build the
cost is the fuel it refuels, so the band is a statement about the ratio of the
delivery price to the fuel price at a fixed burn. Two levers are not free:
scaling both prices together is margin-neutral, and the *distance* premium is
what keeps the far hop worth flying, so it cannot be cut to buy the near hop.

| constant | was | now | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `market.distanceBonus` | 0.5 | 1.7 | the far hop's revenue has to grow with its burn; at 0.5 the reference build's far hop is under water at any fuel price that puts the near hop in band |
| `market.urgencyBonus` | 0.25 | 1.0 | a contract's rate is fixed at posting; at 0.25 the longest-deadline contract paid *less* per kilo than a standing near hop, so the board was full of traps |
| `market.padSpread` | 0.12 | 0.18 | cheap fuel has to be visible from the map for the arbitrage route to exist |
| `market.fragileBonus` | 0.35 | 0.5 | fragile cargo is a real risk with an aggressive profile, so its premium is worth taking |
| verdant prices | 1.4 / 60 | 4.1 / 18 | the ratio, not the level: the near hop lands in band |
| tinmoon prices | 0.9 / 42 | 2.6 / 3.5 | same |
| cinder prices | 2.6 / 130 | 5.5 / 130 | cinder's burn is enormous; only the fuel price moves |
| gossamer prices | 1.1 / 85 | 5.0 / 20 | same |
| `economy.startingCash` | 30000 | 40000 | a reference build plus its first legs |

`constants.parts` is untouched: the wear costs (0.4.3) are what the bands are
meant to include, and retuning them would move the review's measurements.

## What the run says

`experiments/logs/0.4.4-balance.txt`, `node experiments/balance.js 20261004 4 2`
(72 buckets × 8 legs, 1.6 s):

- **Reference build, verdant near hop, standing service: +16.7 %** (p10 +14.8,
  p90 +18.2) — inside +10 … +20 %. The leaner builds on the same hop are the
  scale rows the plan asks for: 1 stage +55.1 %, 2 stages +34.4 %. A light
  payload on a big stack is the other side of the same coin: the reference build
  at 40 kg is −48.9 %, because the burn does not shrink with the cargo.
- **Overbuilt stack (every stage ×1.5 fuel, same payload): −18.1 % on verdant**,
  −21.5 tinmoon, −21.9 cinder, −19.6 gossamer — all ≤ 0 %. It burns ~2× the fuel
  for the same payout.
- **Crash: −$957.** The ledger charges the fuel the leg loaded but not the lost
  structure, so the report prints both the leg's cash and the replacement build
  ($708); the attempt is the sum. A stack loaded to its launch gate with the
  least fuel the catalog allows lifts off and cannot complete the hop.
- **Contract vs standing on one chain**: contract legs +14.5 % (p10 −27.9, p90
  +40.8) against standing legs +4.0 % (p10 +10.8, p90 +20.8) — higher margin and
  a much wider spread, which is what urgency and fragile cargo are for.
- **Leftover fuel**: the return leg of a route flown on what landed books +90.0 %
  against +14.5 % for the same return refuelled. `fuelPolicy: none` has its
  reason.

Two things the long chain shows that the 4-leg buckets cannot, both recorded
here rather than tuned away: a tank reaches `maxFlights` every 12 legs and the
turnaround that follows it rebuilds the stage (~$2.4 k on the verdant
reference), which is why the 33-leg standing mean is +4.0 % while a fresh leg is
+16.7 %; and serving one pad pushes its delivery price down, so a route flown
forever decays. Both push the player to rotate routes rather than to grind one.

## The deck shows the margin

The dispatch card's summary now carries a **PROFIT** row: the payout of every leg
the dispatch would fly, less the autopilot fee and the costs the card already
quotes, as a percentage and a dollar figure — the same number `balance.js`
measures over a chain, for the leg in front of the player. A round trip counts
both legs, and its return leg is priced at the destination for a full load of
fuel, which is the most a refuel there can cost. `render-smoke-test.js` asserts
the row is quoted before the send.
