# Worklog — 0.3.4 Staging authority

## Plan review
- The plan named the hover multiple `minThrustToWeight` (1.7) but did not say
  what it multiplies. Measured three candidates on Cinder, the world with the
  most headroom to get it wrong:
  - **The reserved budget** (`min(thrustReserve * thrustAccel, accelerationCap)`).
    Rejected. Raising 1.7 by the 0.92 reserve and clamping at the cap turns it
    into an effective 1.85 TWR bar, which fires on Cinder's own reference build
    (booster TWR 1.43) and dumps the 1500 kg booster unburned.
  - **The current thrust acceleration, with no burnout test.** Rejected for the
    same reason and a second one: a stage is weakest at light-off, so this
    separates every stage the moment it lights.
  - **The thrust acceleration at burnout** (`fuel = 0`). Kept. Burning only
    sheds mass, so burnout is the most a stage will ever manage — a booster
    that lightens into competence is left to burn, which is what keeps every
    shipped reference route untouched.
- The plan said "a later living stage can" without saying at what mass. Judged
  at the mass the successor would actually have after separation: `payload +
  dry + fuel` of every stage from that index up, with everything below it gone.
  That is the whole point of the trade — the successor inherits a lighter
  rocket, and comparing it at the current mass would never fire.
- The bar is measured against **local** gravity (`gravityAtAltitude`), not
  surface gravity. Measured both on `build-sweep.js 300 1|2|3|5`: local gravity
  is equal or better on delivery counts and produced 0 regressions where
  surface gravity produced 3–4 (tiny two-stage Cinder builds whose booster is
  marginal at the surface and fine a few hundred metres up). Local gravity is
  also the figure the guidance already budgets its braking against
  (`horizontalCapability`), so one number judges both.
- Added two design rules to the plan that the sweep forced out:
  **the successor must carry at least as much fuel as the stage it replaces**
  (an early separation throws the rest of the current tank away, so trading a
  full tank for a smaller one spends more propellant than the authority it
  buys), and the framing of the rule as guidance behaviour rather than an
  envelope limit.
- The plan left the reporting mechanism open; it ships as an `EARLY` label in
  the HUD stage row, set for the one frame the separation is commanded and
  cleared at the top of every update.

## Implemented
- **`js/rocket.js`.** `nextAliveStageIndex(state, from)` replaces the
  `currentStage + 1` assumption in both directions — `activeStage` and
  `separateStage` now both walk forward from a start index, so a separation
  always lands on a live stage and never strands the rocket at
  `currentStage === stageCount`.
  `stackThrustAcceleration(state, index, fuel)` answers the question the rule
  asks: the thrust acceleration the stack would have with the stage at `index`
  burning, everything below it gone, and `fuel` of it still aboard. Both call
  sites are explicit — `0` for the live stage at burnout, the successor's
  current fuel for the candidate.
- **`js/autopilot.js`.** `update()` computes `stageIndex` / `nextStage` once and
  decides staging from `dryStage || earlyStage`, where `earlyStage` is
  `stageOnAuthority && !dryStage && nextStage >= 0 &&` the successor carries at
  least as much fuel `&&` the live stage is below `hoverAuthority` at burnout
  `&&` the successor clears it. `autopilot.stagedEarly` is set only for the
  frame an authority separation is commanded, reset at the top of `update()`,
  in `reset()` and by `setEnabled(false)`.
- **`js/constants.js`.** `autopilot.stageOnAuthority` (default true), so a
  planet or a measurement can hold the rule back without touching the code.
- **`js/render.js`.** Amber `EARLY` beside the stage number while the flag is
  set.
- **`experiments/harness.js`.** `withLimits` restores absent keys by deleting
  them rather than writing `undefined`. Before the fix the first
  `withLimits({})` call left `flight.stageOnAuthority === undefined`, which
  silently disabled the rule for every probe after it — the sweep was
  comparing variants against a baseline that was not the baseline.
- **`experiments/build-sweep.js`.** Now flies a fourth variant, `dry`: limits
  off *and* `stageOnAuthority` false, i.e. the behaviour the game shipped
  before this rule existed. Fixes and regressions are counted against that
  baseline instead of against `off`, so a change to the rule is judged on its
  own rather than on the envelope limits it happens to share.
- **`experiments/envelope.js`.** The `off` variant holds back the envelope
  limits but keeps `stageOnAuthority` in force: it is guidance, not a limit,
  and the reference routes are compared with the rule as shipped.

## Measurements
- **Reference routes unchanged.** `experiments/envelope.js <world> 0
  <balanced|gentle|off>` for all four worlds — 12/12 delivered, and all twelve
  summary lines byte-identical to `experiments/logs/0.3.3-touchdowns.txt`
  (`experiments/logs/0.3.4-touchdowns.txt`). The rule never fires on a shipped
  build: Verdant launches at 1.897 TWR and Cinder's stage 1 reaches 35.01 m/s²
  at burnout against a 28.05 m/s² bar.
- **Build sweep**, 300 builds per planet per variant, seeds 1 / 2 / 3 / 5
  (`experiments/logs/0.3.4-build-sweep.txt`), delivered of 1200:

  | seed | dry | off | balanced | gentle | fixed (off/bal/gentle) | regressions |
  |------|-----|-----|----------|--------|------------------------|-------------|
  | 1 | 1131 | 1152 | 1169 | 1173 | 21 / 38 / 42 | 0 |
  | 2 | 1116 | 1143 | 1157 | 1166 | 27 / 41 / 50 | 0 |
  | 3 | 1122 | 1146 | 1158 | 1166 | 24 / 36 / 44 | 0 |
  | 5 | 1127 | 1159 | 1172 | 1176 | 32 / 46 / 49 | 1 |

  Every variant rises on every seed. The one seed-5 regression (a gossamer
  three-stage build) is present with the rule switched off as well — verified
  by re-running seed 5 with `stageOnAuthority: false` — so it is the
  acceleration limit, not the staging, and the rule breaks nothing on any
  seed.
- **The fuel guard is what buys the zero.** Without it seeds 2 and 3 showed 3
  and 4 regressions, all tiny two-stage Cinder builds whose upper stage is the
  smaller tank: the separation bought hover authority by discarding the range
  the flight needed. With it, the same fixes and no regressions.
- **Traced flight.** On a build whose stage 2 is blind, the guidance lights
  stage 2 at t = 53.0 s, 195 m, T/m 12.5 against a local-gravity bar of 14.8
  and separates at t = 54.0 s, 156 m, where the bar has risen to 15.2. The bar
  is altitude-dependent and self-correcting during descent, which is the
  local-gravity choice working.
- **Mutation suite.** Seven single-line mutations of the rule, each verified to
  fail a test: no burnout guard, rule disabled, last stage droppable,
  next-stage capability ignored, dry tank reported as authority,
  `stageOnAuthority` ignored, surface instead of local gravity. Plus the
  dropped fuel guard and the dropped HUD marker. `experiments/flight-test.js`
  covers the new `rocket.js` helpers (light-off vs burnout, a separated stage
  skipped, a later stage judged on the stack it would inherit).
- **Suites.** `coords`, `aerodynamics`, `flight`, `builder`, `render-smoke`,
  `autopilot`, `trajectory` all pass; `node --check` clean on every `js/` and
  `experiments/` script; tabs, LF, no trailing whitespace, no long lines.

## Also noted, not changed
- `planets.copyInto` shares the preset's `flight` object by reference between
  worlds that use the same preset. Harmless today — the harness restores every
  key it mutates in a `finally` — but a latent hazard for anything that tunes a
  world in place. Deep-copying the block is a small follow-up, not needed here.
- `render.drawFlightTelemetry` is only reached while `flight` is non-null.
