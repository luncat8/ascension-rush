# Worklog — 0.5.1 Damage & failures

## Starting point

0.4.4 done: pad markets, the contract board, the parts catalog, service and
wear, and a balance pass whose every band passes. The 0.3 physics baseline is
fixed and measured — 48 reference route rows byte-identical to
`experiments/logs/0.3.5-touchdowns.txt`, `build-sweep 300 1` at
1116/1143/1157/1166 with 0 regressions, 868 assertions green. The 0.5 plan's
damage half was unstarted: `stage.stress`, `parts.repairValue` and
`flight.repairCost` existed as the 0.4.3 hooks, and every part's `repairPerKg`
was 0 so the bill could never be anything but 0.

## Plan review

`0.5-plan.md` is now two increments with the damage half written out in full.
Three places where the shipped model leaves the sketch, all of them measured
rather than assumed:

- **Limits come from the world's own certified envelope**, not from guessed
  constants. A stage is rated at `planet.flight.maxThrustAcceleration` /
  `maxDynamicPressure` × `structureMargin` × `strength`. That is what makes one
  table of constants work on four worlds whose nominal peaks run from 0 kPa to
  62 kPa and from 16 to 32 m/s², and it ties the damage model to a number the
  player already meets in the autopilot's limits.
- **Overload is not itself a failure, it is what adds to the stress that fails
  later.** The sketch's `rate = k * (stress / stressLimit)^n` was tried with the
  instantaneous excess as the driver and it is unusable: the reference build at
  0.85 and a skimpy one at 0.5 differ by a factor of ~1.7 in load, so a cubic
  in the excess makes a fresh skimpy stack fail 85 % of the time and a
  well-built one never, with nothing in between. Stress is the driver, the
  exponent is 2, and the overload feeds it.
- **There is no choice to fly damaged.** The repair is charged on the leg that
  flies the stack, like the overhaul, and a stack that cannot afford it does not
  launch. The decisions left to the player are how strongly to build, which
  profile to fly, and when to overhaul. Flying damaged would need a deck toggle
  and a reason to take the risk; that is 0.5.2's or a later increment's call,
  and the model has the room for it.

The plan's per-part stress (engine / tank / payload as separate accumulators)
is one `stress` per stage instead, with the part prices keeping the
distinction where it costs money: `repairValue` bills the stress against each
part's own `repairPerKg`, and the payload takes its own damage channel because
the payout is the only thing it can lose.

## Implemented

- **`js/damage.js`** (new). `step(game, dt, accel, pressure)` accumulates the
  load every attached stage is carrying, rolls the failures of the stage that is
  burning, drains a leak, and ruptures a tank that is past its limit with
  propellant aboard. `landing`/`applyLanding` measure the arrival against the
  reference band, `separation` charges the stack for a staging event, and
  `alarmLabel`/`failureText` are what the HUD, the debrief and the log read.
  Sampling is `1 - exp(-rate * dt)` against a mulberry32 seeded per flight, so
  a step change cannot move the odds and the same flight fails the same way
  twice. The tuning lives in `constants.damage`.
- **`js/physics.js`** measures the step's non-gravitational load (thrust and
  drag, gravity added back) and hands it to the model at the end of the step —
  after the ground crossing, so a rupture cannot be raised by a step that has
  already landed.
- **`js/mission.js`**: `touchdown` and the new `rupture` both build their record
  through one `flightResult` and end the flight through one `finish`, so a
  landing, a crash and a loss of vehicle differ only in what they measured.
  Landing stress is applied *before* the stage state is captured, which is what
  makes the log and the repair bill describe the stack that actually came down.
  The payout is `quote * (1 - payloadDamage)`.
- **`js/parts.js`**: `scrapped` is worn-out-or-wrecked, so a stage past
  `scrapStress` is replaced by the turnaround instead of repaired;
  `stageRepairValue` adds a whole new engine for one that died; `applyRepair`
  clears the damage the leg paid for.
- **`js/operations.js` / `js/economy.js`**: the repair is quoted by
  `evaluateLeg` (so the dispatch card, the PROFIT row and `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`
  all see it), charged by `economy.repair` on the leg that flies, and counted in
  `flight.cashDelta` — it was being charged without being counted, which is why
  the first damage-on balance run showed the same margins with less cash.
- **`js/autopilot.js`**: a dead engine is a staging condition of its own
  (`deadStage`), next to dry and authority staging.
- **`js/render.js` / `js/deck.js`**: `STRUCTURE` (a bar against the scrap
  threshold) and `ALARM` rows in flight; the dispatch card quotes the repair
  and the service line names the damage and a dead engine; the log gains a
  `DAMAGE` row and a `CARGO ARRIVED` row.

## The tuning

`experiments/logs/0.5.1-failures.txt`, `node experiments/failure.js 120 20261005`
(8 buckets × 120 legs × 2 tables, 39 s). A fresh stack, one hop, per world and
build strength:

| strength | verdant | tinmoon | cinder | gossamer | repair per hop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.00 | 0.0 % | 1.7 % | 0.8 % | 0.0 % | $14–50 |
| 0.85 (reference) | 0.0 % | 0.0 % | 0.8 % | 0.0 % | $19–68 |
| 0.75 | 0.0 % | 0.0 % | 0.0 % | 0.0 % | $25–87 |
| 0.50 | 20.0 % | 66.7 % | 10.0 % | 18.3 % | $104–362 |

The plan asks for ~15–30 % of some failure per Verdant hop at strength 0.5 and
a low rate at 0.75; verdant is in band, cinder and gossamer are at the bottom
of it. **Tinmoon is the outlier at 67 %** and was left there deliberately: its
hop takes 85 s against verdant's 45 s and the model is, correctly, paid in
load-seconds, so the world with the longest flights is the one where a skimpy
build does not survive. The same table with engines at 90 % of their rated
burn time runs 7–21 % at strength 1.0 and 18–29 % at 0.5, which is the number
that makes an overhaul worth buying before the gate forces it.

Two constants moved during the tuning and are worth recording:

- `stressRate` 0.03 → 0.06 with `failureRate` 0.12 → 0.10 and the exponent 3 →
  2. The first model failed a fresh skimpy stack 85 % of the time and a
  reference one never, because the cubic was reading the instantaneous
  overload; the second reads the stress the flight has actually accumulated.
- `wearRate` 0.02 → 0.004. At 0.02 an engine at 90 % of its rating failed on
  95 % of hops and crashed on most of them, which made the overhaul the only
  rational move at any price and left the player no decision at all.

## What it costs

`experiments/logs/0.5.1-balance-damage.txt`,
`node experiments/balance.js 20261004 4 2 damage`, against the same command
without the last argument. Every 0.4.4 band still passes:

| band | damage off | damage on | target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reference build, verdant near hop | +16.1 % | +14.9 % | +10 … +20 % |
| steady state, verdant / tinmoon / cinder / gossamer | +13.2 / +16.2 / +13.2 / +16.2 % | +12.0 / +14.2 / +12.5 / +15.4 % | +10 … +20 % |
| overbuilt stack, verdant | −18.1 % | −20.0 % | ≤ 0 % |
| contract legs vs standing | +24.7 % vs +10.6 % | +23.7 % vs +9.3 % | higher and wider |

So the model costs a service 1–2 points of margin and a few percent of a hard
hop, which is the size it has to be to be a cost of doing business rather than
the whole game. The crash band is unchanged at −$957: a wreck is written off,
not repaired.

## Repaired on the way

`experiments/balance.js` reports the contract board against a standing service,
and its "contract legs" row had been empty since the payload-ladder change
moved the board's rungs off the reference payload: it only ever looked for a
contract asking for exactly the build's nominal cargo. It now takes the
heaviest open contract on the hop the build can lift, and the band is
measurable again (+24.7 % against +10.6 % standing, higher and wider, as 0.4.4
recorded).

## Validation

- All 12 suites pass (967 assertions; 63 of them are the new
  `experiments/damage-test.js`, the rest are the damage rows added to the
  operations, service, render and parts suites): the rate's shape, the sampling's independence
  from the step (60 fps against 240 fps), strength scaling, all four failure
  modes and their effects, an accelerating leak, rupture, soft/hard/fragile
  touchdowns, the autopilot staging on a dead engine, the same seed rolling the
  same flight, and damage off reproducing the 0.4 model exactly.
- Physics did not move. `envelope.js` reproduces all 12 reference-route
  summaries and the verdant table byte for byte, `build-sweep 300 1` is
  unchanged at 1116/1143/1157/1166 with 0 regressions, and `impact-accuracy
  12 7` reproduces its table. Those scripts fly with damage off — the harness
  switches it off for every baseline measurement, and `failure.js` switches it
  back on for the one that is about it.
- `balance.js` is unchanged by 0.5.1 with damage off (byte-identical to a run
  from the pre-0.5.1 tree), and the damage-on run is recorded.
- `render-smoke-test.js` asserts the HUD draws both new rows, names a failure
  on the alarm row, and clears it when the alarm expires. It also flies a leg
  through the real frame loop to its end, then sends a damaged stack from the
  deck and checks the whole repair path: the service line quotes it, the
  dispatch counts it, the flight record carries it, and the stack was serviced
  before it launched.
- `operations-test.js` asserts a crash and a rupture are logged with the cargo
  damage and cause they carry, and that a written-off stack is never billed a
  repair.

## Next step

**0.5.2 Competitor pressure.** The market's `servedPressure` is still only the
player's own volume. Give the other pads a rival operator serving the same
destination — bidding on the same board, flying the same autopilot, and priced
into the same drift — and let the player see it in the market tab and buy the
route back. The 0.5.1 repair numbers are the cost base it has to beat, and the
0.4.4 saturation numbers are the baseline it has to move.
