# Plan 0.4 — Economy Balance, Market & Parts

Subject scope: tune the cost model so that delivery runs are profitable
when flown well, unprofitable if over-built or crashed; introduce proper
parts (engines, tanks, payload fairing) with cost, service life and repair
cost; add a market screen where prices fluctuate per pad and per flight so
the player chooses *what* to deliver and *where*, not just "fly to target".

This plan also provides the numbers that 0.3 autopilot and 0.5 damage use
(repair cost, part survival) but does not implement failures itself.

## Files
```
js/parts.js         # part catalog & per-stage bill of materials
js/market.js        # pad-specific prices, contract board, price drift
js/economy.js       # reworked: balance constants, profit math
js/builder.js       # shows per-part cost, maintenance, expected margin
js/hud.js           # market tab, cash flow breakdown

experiments/balance.js  # node script: sim thousands of flights, tune constants
```

## Starting numbers (measured on the 0.3.1 reference flights)
`experiments/envelope.js` flies each planet's reference build over every
directed route with the autopilot and reports time, flight envelope, peak
altitude / speed and fuel left. These post-aero runs replace the older 0.3.0
baseline for balance work; re-derive prices from them rather than from guesswork.

```
world      standard hop   fuel left at hop   long route   dry mass   peak Q (AoA at peak Q)
Verdant    5.0 km, 44.6 s  512 of 880 kg      10.0 km, 62.5 s  666 kg   32.2 kPa (27.7°)
Tinmoon    2.6 km, 46.6 s  322 of 440 kg       8.0 km, 82.8 s  423 kg    0.0 kPa (n/a)
Cinder     6.0 km, 47.6 s 1326 of 2800 kg    12.0 km, 68.0 s 1725 kg   79.6 kPa (36.1°)
Gossamer   5.2 km, 52.9 s  448 of 800 kg      11.7 km, 94.6 s  621 kg   66.2 kPa (9.2°)
```

Peak Q and its concurrent AoA are the maximums across every directed route
for the reference build; AoA is not a load metric on the airless Tinmoon. See
[`archive/0.3.1-worklog.md`](./archive/0.3.1-worklog.md) for peak AoA paired
with its own Q sample and the route-level report.

Two consequences for the cost model below: the reference build lands with
half its fuel still aboard, so *over-building is the normal mistake* and the
estimates must make the wasted fuel and structure visible; and a single
`$60/kg` delivery price pays roughly twice the fuel cost of a hop, which is
too flat to distinguish a well-flown run from a wasteful one.

## Cost model, revised
Separate cash flow from operating margin so reusable parts are neither
charged at full price on every flight nor treated as free:
```
flight cash delta = reward
                  - fuel consumed
                  - new parts bought for this build
                  - repair and service fees
                  - autopilot fee
operating margin  = reward
                  - fuel cost
                  - part wear allowance
                  - repair/service fees
                  - autopilot fee
part wear allowance = replacement cost / expected service life
```
- `reward = payloadMass * pricePerKg_deliver_local * distanceBonus * urgencyBonus`.
  `distanceBonus` grows sub-linearly with wrap-distance (e.g. `1 + 0.5 * (dx/C)`);
  `urgencyBonus` is set per contract.
- Charge fuel per kg actually burned at the *launch pad's* price, matching
  the 0.2 ledger; unused loaded fuel is not a consumed-fuel expense.
  Different pads can have different fuel prices — it pays to tank up cheap.
- Parts bought in the builder persist in the hangar and incur cash cost once;
  the wear allowance is used for estimates and balance experiments, not booked
  again as a cash expense. `new_parts_cost` is described under Parts.
- `repair_cost` is 0 in 0.4 but the field exists and is reserved for 0.5.
- `service_fee` is a flat pad fee (small, e.g. covers pad rental).
- Contract urgency is measured in dispatches/contract-board turns, not wall
  clock time, so opening the builder never silently expires a mission.

### Steel vs. cost
Steel is no longer a single $/kg number. Instead the structural mass of a
stage is broken into parts (below), each with its own $/kg and service
properties.

## Parts
Rockets are built from a small catalog of virtual parts (no sprite/model
detail — these are parameteric, like KSP part configs):

### Part types
- **Engine** (`type: 'engine'`): `{ thrustMax, ispSea, ispVac, mass, cost,
  maxBurns, maxThrottleTime, reliability }`. Mass and cost are fixed per
  engine; attach one per stage. After `maxThrottleTime` seconds of
  cumulative burn time across flights the engine requires overhaul (paid
  service cost) before next use. `reliability` (0..1) scales failure
  probability in 0.5.
- **Tank** (`type: 'tank'`): `{ fuelMax, dryMass, cost, strength }`.
  One tank per stage; dryMass scales with fuelMax (regression table:
  small, medium, large variants so bigger tanks have better mass ratio).
  `strength` is the 0.2 build-quality slider realized as picking a
  lighter-but-fragile vs heavier-but-tough tank.
- **Fairing** (`type: 'fairing'`, optional, on top stage): `{ mass, cost,
  maxDragVy }`. Reduces drag coefficient in atmosphere for payload; jettisoned
  automatically above a certain altitude (small one-time event). Absent in
  0.4 render (no visual), but affects physics and cost.
- **Payload** is not a part; it's the delivery mass, priced by the
  contract.

### Per-stage bill of materials
```
stage = {
  engine: partEngine,
  tank:   partTank,
  fairing: partFairing | null,   // only top stage
  fuelMass, fuelMax,
  strength,                      // applied to tank selection
  // maintenance
  engineBurnTimeUsed,            // seconds; compared to engine.maxThrottleTime
}
```
Total dry mass of a stage = `engine.mass + tank.dryMass + (fairing?.mass || 0)`.

### Parts persistence
The player's "hangar" persists across flights (on `game` in memory; 0.4
doesn't need to serialize to disk but keeps it in a single object for
easy save/load later). Each launch uses existing parts unless the player
buys new ones (or replaces worn ones) in the builder. So overbuilding is
costly in two ways: you paid for bigger parts upfront, and heavier rockets
burn more fuel.

### Service cost
- After each flight, landings accumulate stress (see 0.5). A soft landing
  adds `engineBurnTimeUsed` to the engine; a hard-but-not-crash landing
  adds repair cost proportional to `(vy - vSafe)` on engines and tanks.
- Overhaul: when `engineBurnTimeUsed >= maxThrottleTime`, the engine is
  marked "needs service" and blocks launch until the overhaul fee
  (`engine.cost * overhaulFactor`, e.g. 0.2) is paid.
- Tanks have a `maxFlights` rating; beyond that they must be replaced.

## Market
Each pad has a price table that drifts gently between flights:
```
pad.prices = {
  fuel,                // $/kg
  deliverPerKg,        // $/kg reward base
  steel,               // $/kg for buying new tanks/engines
  serviceFlat          // pad fee
}
```
Between flights, nudge each price with a small bounded random walk
(persisted per pad, wrap-aware). The contract board at a pad lists:
- **Destination pad** (any other pad).
- **Payload mass** (range of small/medium/large).
- **Reward per kg** (destination pad's `deliverPerKg` × urgency).
- **Urgency** (time-to-expire in flights or "immediate"; immediate pays more
  but you must take off right away, no extra build time).
- **Fragility flag** (high-value low-g cargo: pays more but stricter landing
  vSafe / max-G — hooks into 0.5 damage).

Contracts are generated from the destination's prices plus a small random
spread, so the player learns to arbitrage: take high-paying cargo to a
pad that has cheap fuel for the return leg.

## Balance tuning
Goal: a correctly-sized rocket doing a short hop on Verdant yields ~+10–20 %
profit margin; a 3-stage over-built rocket doing the same hop yields ~0 %
or a loss; a crash yields a big loss; longer routes and fragile cargo
yield higher margin but higher risk.

Tuning approach:
- Start with physics-based defaults:
  - Fuel mass ratio (fuelMax / (fuelMax+dryMass)) ~ 0.8–0.9 for good tanks.
  - Isp ~ 300 s sea / 350 s vac for a mid-tier engine.
  - Thrust-to-weight at launch ≥ 1.3.
- Use `experiments/balance.js` to Monte Carlo:
  - For each preset × each distance bracket × each stage count, simulate
    1000 autopilot flights with randomized builder choices (payload %,
    overbuild %).
  - Record Δcash distribution.
  - Adjust unit prices (`PRICE_FUEL_BASE`, `PRICE_ENGINE_T40`, etc.) until
    the profit-margin histogram peaks in the target band for well-built
    rockets and is negative for overbuilt/crashed ones.
- Keep the tuned constants as named values in `js/economy.js` (not magic
  numbers inline).

## Builder UI updates
- Parts picker per stage: engine dropdown (small/medium/large), tank size
  dropdown (S/M/L), fairing toggle for the top stage.
- Cost breakdown panel:
  - New parts cost (if replacing worn parts vs. reusing).
  - Estimated fuel cost (using current pad fuel price and autopilot
    estimate of fuel needed, or player's own history).
  - Service/overhaul fees.
  - Expected reward.
  - Expected margin (reward - all costs), colored green/red.
- Warning states: insufficient dV (red), overbuilt (yellow), worn parts
  (amber).

## Acceptance criteria for 0.4
- A player running the Verdant short-hop contract with a properly sized
  rocket can consistently make a profit (~10–20 % margin) using either
  manual flight or autopilot.
- Overbuilding (too many stages, oversized engines, over-full tanks) shows
  red/amber warnings and yields negative margin in the Monte Carlo.
- Part wear works across flights: after enough burns, an engine demands
  overhaul and blocks launch until paid; replacing it costs full price.
- Market prices differ between pads and drift between flights; at least one
  obvious arbitrage route exists (cheap fuel on one pad, high delivery
  price on another).
- The `experiments/balance.js` script runs under node (no browser) and
  prints a profit-margin summary per preset/distance/stage-count bucket
  that matches the tuning goal.
- No per-frame allocations added by market/parts code; economy math runs
  only at build / launch / landing events, not during flight.
