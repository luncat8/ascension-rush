# Findings, pitfalls & useful techniques

- Keep flight `wx` unbounded; store pad longitudes canonically and use positive modulo for their periodic screen copies. JavaScript `%` is a remainder, not mathematical modulo, for negative inputs.
- At a viewport that spans a whole planet circumference, a physical rocket's horizontal dimensions are sub-pixel. Draw its orientation as a small screen-space glyph while projecting its world position; do not distort it through the world transform.
- Keep the surface line fixed at the top of the soil strip when using an absolute logarithmic altitude map. A vertical camera translation would move the ground strip and invalidate the inverse mapping.
- Aim the flight-control nose from the rocket's screen-space position to the cursor; unprojecting through log-Y makes mouse angles disagree with the visible heading glyph. Use that heading as the world thrust vector, and retain the fixed-step integrator in world units.
- Landing tolerances must account for the wrap-map's horizontal compression. On a 1,000 km circumference shown one-to-one across the viewport, a 50 m pad radius is effectively invisible; choose and render a kilometer-scale gameplay radius or add zoom before tightening it.
- Compare a builder's Tsiolkovsky readout with the ideal staged rocket equation, not with flight delta-v after gravity and drag losses. Full mission range depends on the player's steering and coast/burn profile.
- Quote exhaust velocity as `Isp * g_standard` (9.80665), never `Isp * local g`. Isp in seconds is already a specific impulse normalized by standard gravity; multiplying it by a moon's surface gravity makes engines burn several times too slowly, which reads as "the rocket is sluggish and the fuel lasts forever" until the flight simply runs out of world.
- When a world is drawn from the whole circumference in one viewport, world scale is a *gameplay* parameter. If pads have to be reached in 15-30 s, shrink the planet instead of inflating the rocket: circumference sets the leg length, the pixel-per-metre ratio sets whether a pad is a visible target, and fuel/pacing follow from both.
- A velocity-profile tracker lags a moving profile by roughly `accel / gain` m/s, which is exactly the overshoot a landing cannot tolerate. Feed the profile's own deceleration forward (or track the stopping distance directly) instead of raising the gain; a high gain on a saturated actuator only trades overshoot for oscillation. Both channels need this: on the horizontal axis the lag becomes a pad overshoot, on the descent axis it becomes a crash.
- Gate a feed-forward term by the condition it is derived from. The descent feed-forward (`descentMargin² * stoppingAccel`) is only valid while the sink rate actually sits on the descent profile; applied unconditionally it becomes a permanent upward bias that pushes the rocket above its cruise altitude and costs fuel and seconds on every flight.
- Thrust can only push, so the *downward* authority of a rocket is its local gravity alone. A vertical controller that assumes symmetric authority overshoots its altitude target on a low-gravity world and then cannot come down any faster than free fall; size the climb rate against the weaker direction.
- Never clamp the *thrust angle* when the vertical channel is load-bearing: fixing the angle while the horizontal error dominates steals the thrust that holds the rocket up. Build the requested acceleration vector (gravity compensation plus both axis corrections) and clip its magnitude; cap the horizontal component at `sqrt(budget² - vertical²)`.
- A climb profile must be bounded by what the descent profile can cancel. An uncapped `sqrt(2*g*dh)` climb rate out-runs the glide slope, tops the rocket out kilometres above its cruise altitude, and spends the landing reserve on the way back down. A `climbMargin` factor below 1 keeps the two profiles consistent.
- Time scale belongs in the simulation *budget*, not the integration step. Accumulate `frameDt * timeScale` and drain it in fixed steps; scaling `dt` instead changes the integrator's behaviour and makes the same flight end differently at 4x than at 0.5x.
- When a whole-flush-to-zero model of a value is replaced, every reader has to be audited: fuel-left telemetry that recomputed `loadedFuel - fuelUsed` silently reported 0.0 kg once the accounting moved to per-stage masses. Sum the live state instead of deriving it from a second, stale source of truth.
- A speed target with a constant offset (`|dx| * gain + 2`) never reaches zero and flips sign as the position error crosses zero, so the rocket hunts a few m/s either side of the pad for the whole landing. With the vertical channel in free fall (a low-gravity world) that chatter owns the heading, the nose swings through *straight down* with thrust on, and which routes crash then depends on the frame rate. Let position-term speed targets decay smoothly to zero.
- Ignition resets the throttle: `applyBuild` and `separateStage` set it to 1 and the shared slew limiter needs about 0.4 s to bring it down, so an acceleration cap measured as a peak hides behind a light-off spike (caps of 16, 18 and 20 m/s² all read as the same 20.5 m/s² "floor" on Verdant, which was just the second stage lighting at full throttle). Light the next stage at the throttle the autopilot holds, and measure the thrust *applied* (`thrust / mass`), not the available `T / m`.
- Give every acceleration figure the same mass (the start-of-step mass the guidance uses). Using the integrator's step-average mass for one of them made a cap look broken by 0.005% and a test with a 1e-9 tolerance fail for a reason that was only bookkeeping.
- A thrust-acceleration cap needs a floor tied to local gravity. At 1.45 x surface gravity on Cinder the vertical channel (weight plus descent control) consumed the whole budget, the brake profile still planned about 15 m/s² of sideways braking against the roughly 8 m/s² left, and the rocket overshot the pad and climbed back 140 m; at 1.6 x and above the landings were clean. Resolve limits against gravity once per flight instead of hand-tuning each profile on each world.
- Fading only the *forward* thrust at a pressure cap is cheap and well behaved (peak Q settles at 0.82–0.92 x the cap, costs 0–4% time, and cuts a hot build's peak Q by about 70% for 13–16% more time), but it removes the along-flow part and keeps the lift part, so the thrust axis tilts toward vertical and the angle of attack rises as Q approaches the cap. An angle-of-attack clamp inside the same band fights that: the sideways thrust it forbids is the thrust holding the rocket up, so the rocket sinks into denser air (q·sin α got worse, 19.9 to 24 kPa) or keeps a forward component the fade had removed. Keep it a backstop that eases in across the band.
- When clamping a heading to the nearer end of a symmetric axis, requests near "sideways" are a tie, and a tie that flips with noise swings the nose through broadside (the rate-limited actuator then dwells at 90° angle of attack). Keep the axis on the end it already points at until the request is clearly on the other side. And because the throttle is the *projection* of the request on the permitted axis, a sideways request delivers `budget * sin²(angle)` of lift, so the weight-support floor is `asin(sqrt(margin * g / budget))`, not `asin(margin * g / budget)`.
- Count a constraint's oscillation by how often its limiter flag switches (Q switched at most twice per flight: on in cruise, off at the brake), not by the thrust axis crossing the airflow axis. At near-zero throttle the axis sits sideways to the flow and crosses it on the slightest drift, and the guidance's proportional term already dithers its heading sign about zero while coasting down the brake profile (37 command sign changes in 15 s with no limits at all).
- Test guidance in the frame loop's order (one control update per frame, then physics) at several frame rates and time scales; 30 fps at 4x is a 0.133 s decision step. A harness that updates the controller every fixed step hides frame-rate dependence, which here only showed as different Tinmoon routes crashing at different settings.
- Mutation-check new tests: break each mechanism on a backup copy (no fade, no cap, no clamp, no floor, no hysteresis, full-throttle ignition) and confirm a named assertion fails. Several first drafts passed or failed for the wrong reason, such as probing a state where the guidance was already braking (so there was no forward thrust to fade) or sizing a threshold from the build's peak thrust instead of its launch thrust.
- Random-build sweeps (stage count, fuel, payload, route, frame rate) find gaps the reference builds hide: a middle stage whose `T / m` is below local gravity cannot hover after staging, and the dry-stage-only staging rule never brings the strong upper stage in. Limits change *when* that happens, so a few marginal builds flip each way; judge a change by flights fixed against flights broken, compared with the same flights under no limits.
- A tuning cache keyed on the planet alone ignores a profile change. Key it on everything the resolution reads (planet and profile) and drop it when a flight ends, so edited limits are picked up and nothing is re-resolved inside the update loop.
- Give a predictor the live integrator's own step and formulas instead of a second, coarser model. A coast predictor at 1/30 s carried 150 m of range error on a dense-air coast (1.7% of range, over tolerance); the same code at the live 1/120 s step kept all 1,176 tested states inside 1% / 25 m for 0.1 ms a forecast, and it makes the equivalence with an engine-off `physics.step` flight exact enough to assert to 1e-9 m. Two step sizes of *one* integrator also gives the validation reference for free: run it 24x finer and the only difference left is step size, never model.
- On a periodic map, project every marker through the periodic copy of its longitude. The coast marker projected the raw impact longitude, so a long westward coast "left the screen" at ground the player could see, while pads already went through `coords.nearestPeriodicX`. A wrap-aware *error* (`wrapDelta` against the pad) and a wrap-aware *screen position* are two separate fixes; missing either shows up only on some routes.
- Bound a forecast by a horizon and publish "invalid", never the last good point: a stale marker reads as a promise. The HUD then owes the player a reason (`OUT OF HORIZON`), otherwise a hidden marker looks like a broken feature.
- Validate a vacuum trajectory with an exact invariant, not a parabola. On a world whose radius is 2.5 km, a constant-`g` analytic check is wrong by 15% at 500 m; energy conservation in the inverse-square field (`v² = v₀² + 2GM(1/r₁ − 1/r₀)`, `GM = g·R²`) is exact and tests the integrator instead of the approximation.
- The most useful debrief numbers are often already computed: `physics.step` interpolates the ground crossing, so the velocities at touchdown are measured, not estimated. Quote them with a signed wrap-aware pad error (`+` = east) and a crash becomes diagnosable — "1.2 km west of Farport, 40.0 m/s down" says what "CRASH" does not.
- When mutation-checking a test suite, break the mechanism the assertion names, not just the first assertion that fails. Removing drag was caught by the integrator-equivalence test long before the drag assertions ran; removing it from `physics.step` as well proved the drag assertions were live too.
- Judge a "can this stage land the rocket?" threshold at the stage's **burnout** figure, not its current one, and compare it against **thrust acceleration** rather than the reserved budget. Burning only sheds mass, so burnout is the most a stage will ever manage and a booster that lightens into competence is correctly left to burn; the reserved budget folds in a 0.92 reserve and an acceleration cap, which turned a 1.7 TWR rule into an effective 1.85 one and dumped Cinder's own 1.43 TWR booster unburned.
- Compare the successor at the mass it would actually have. A stage's worth is not its thrust but `thrust / mass` on the rocket it would inherit, so a candidate must be weighed with everything below it gone; comparing it at the current mass makes the rule never fire.
- Prefer **local** gravity over surface gravity for an altitude-dependent threshold, and measure the alternative rather than assuming. Here local gravity was equal or better on delivery counts and had 0 regressions where surface gravity had 3-4 on tiny two-stage builds on the heavy worlds, and it is the same figure the guidance already budgets its braking against.
- An early separation is a *trade*, not a diagnosis: it discards the rest of the current tank, so the successor has to carry at least as much fuel as the stage it replaces. Without that guard a two-stage build whose upper stage is the smaller tank traded away the range it needed for hover authority it barely lacked — the guard cost no fixes and removed every regression on two of four seeds.
- Restoring a shared config object has to restore its **shape**, not just its values. A harness that saved `flight[key]` and wrote `undefined` back for absent keys left an explicit `undefined` behind, which then overrode the module default with nothing and silently disabled a boolean flag for every later probe. Delete absent keys on restore.
- A before/after baseline has to be the *whole* behaviour being changed, not just the limits. Comparing a new staging rule against "limits off" credits (or blames) it for every difference the envelope limits also cause; fly a fourth variant with the new flag off and the limits off, and count fixes and regressions against that.
- When a behaviour is guidance rather than a limit, a measurement that turns limits off must turn the flag *on*. `envelope.js`'s `off` variant holds back the envelope limits but keeps `stageOnAuthority` in force, otherwise the reference routes are compared against a flight the game never ships.
- Never assume `currentStage + 1` is the next stage that burns. One helper (`nextAliveStageIndex(state, from)`) used by both `activeStage` and `separateStage` makes a separation always land on a live stage, so the last stage cannot be stranded and a dead stage cannot be lit.
- Charge consumables when they are **loaded**, not when the flight ends. Pricing turnaround and refuel at touchdown left no moment at which a "do not refuel, fly what landed" policy could be expressed; moving both charges to leg preparation made the policy a one-line branch and made the leg that pays for them visible in the ledger.
- When a plan's state machine and its cost model disagree, resolve it by finding the moment the charge has to land and letting the state machine follow. A resting `turnaround` status plus touchdown-time charges looked tidy and made one required behaviour impossible; dropping the status cost nothing.
- A view that renders from a shared evaluator must consume the object that evaluator **returns**. A module-local `createStats()` left in place while the real numbers moved to `operations.typeStats` read all zeros, so every rocket design silently showed capacity 0 and clamped its payload to 0 — no error, just a wrong number on screen.
- Read form inputs only in the input handler. An `update()` that called `readInputs()` first overwrote every programmatically loaded draft (open a type, apply planet defaults, switch planet) with the previous form values on the very first repaint.
- Gate a per-frame scheduler on a revision counter bumped by commands, and evaluate into one reusable scratch object. Then the deck can ask "is this route ready?" every frame without allocating anything in the render path, and the scheduler cannot fire twice for one state change.
- A "dismissed" flag with no expiry is a veto that outlives its reason. Clear it in every command that expresses intent (dispatch, build, create/edit/delete, enable, toggle, retype) *and* when the dismissed thing stops being ready, or the player edits a route and it never offers again.
- Availability queries must exclude terminal states. Counting a `lost` rocket as present made a pad whose only rocket had crashed report `ROCKET BUSY` instead of `NO ROCKET AT <PAD>` — the generic reason the plan explicitly forbade.
- Drive UI tests from the real markup with a small forgiving HTML tokenizer instead of hand-built fake nodes. An element id that the code reaches for but the markup omits then fails a test; with hand-built nodes it throws at runtime in the browser instead.
- Pick the fixture that actually exercises the mechanism. The 5 km reference hop lands on its booster without staging, so a "the separated stages came back" assertion passed vacuously until the test moved to the 10 km route, which really does shed a stage.
- Never leave `assert.ok(expr || true)` as a placeholder: replace it with the measured quantity. The return-blocked case is now the surviving stack's launch TWR (0.900) against the 1.15 bar, quoted next to the 2.576 it had before the separations.
- A per-row measurement table is a stronger regression guard than a pass count. Recording all 48 reference routes in 4 worlds x 3 profiles and byte-comparing it with the previous version proves nothing moved anywhere; "48/48 delivered" would have hidden a slower, hotter or heavier flight that still landed.
- A shared scratch config must copy **every** field the callee reads, not just the ones the caller was thinking about. `operations.typeStats` copied `fuelMass`/`strength` into its scratch stages but not the part ids, so every number derived from it (dry mass, thrust, dV, payload limit, the build price) was measured with the catalog defaults whatever the player had picked — while a sibling function that read the type directly (`typeTwr`) showed the real parts, so one panel contradicted itself. When a measurement is funnelled through a scratch buffer, adding a field to the model means auditing the copy, and a test has to compare the funnel's output against the direct formula.
- A function that returns a shared record cannot be read twice. `typeStats` hands back the same object every call, so `var a = typeStats(x); var b = typeStats(y); a.dryMass` is `y`'s number. Read the numbers out into locals before the next call, or the test asserts against a value that moved.
- Price a thing once, in one unit, and let every money figure be that unit times a price. Two parallel formulas (structure mass x steel here, costPerMass folded there) meant a part's `costPerMass` existed in the catalog and reached no ledger: the function that folded it was called only from its own test. One `structureValue` in steel-kg, quoted by build, turnaround and capital-at-risk alike, makes the default (1.0) reproduce the old money exactly and makes an alternative's premium real.
- Dead code that only a test calls is a missing feature, not coverage. `grep` for the function outside `js/` before believing a plan bullet that says it is wired.
- A part attached to another part has to be released with it. A fairing modelled as whole-rocket state survived the separation of the stage carrying it, so drag stayed scaled by a fairing that had already left with the stage and the later jettison subtracted its mass a second time from a dead stage. Model the attachment as a condition the physics step re-checks (`fairingAttached && topStage.alive`), not as a one-time flag.
- When a leg can be blocked by a service the player must authorize, the view has to evaluate **with the authorization it is displaying**. The maintenance button set `form.overhaul` and the card kept calling `evaluateLeg` without it, so the button appeared to do nothing; only a DOM-level test that clicked the button and re-read the send button caught it. Assert the click's effect on the *other* control, not on the flag.
- `game.flight` is released on touchdown (`mission.touchdown` sets it to null), so a test that flies a leg must capture what it needs from the flight record before the loop ends, or read the log entry the leg wrote. Asserting on `game.flight.x` after `fly()` throws on `null`, and reading a field the debrief does not carry (`lastReport.turnaroundCost`) fails as `undefined` — both look like a broken feature rather than a test bug.
- Wear that accrues in the physics step should be a number on state that already exists, added in the branch that already runs. `stage.engineBurnTimeUsed += dt` inside the existing burn branch costs nothing per frame; a wear object, an array or a callback there would allocate in the hot path.
- Counting seconds only while burning makes burn time exceed `flight.elapsed` by up to one step: the touchdown step adds `dt * crossingFraction` to elapsed but the full `dt` to the burn. Assert `burn <= elapsed + fixedStep`, not `burn < elapsed`.
