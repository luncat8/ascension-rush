# Worklog — 0.3 autopilot, planet presets & the flight-scale rebalance

## Review
- Re-read the 0.2 slice with fresh eyes. It flew, but the numbers were wrong for
  the stated goal: a 1,000 km world with pads 250 km apart and one-rocket-for-
  every-world tuning. The builder could not make a rocket that reached another
  pad (measured: a 50 km leg arrived at 195 s with the tank dry and crashed;
  250 km was hopeless), and a "flight" was a two-minute fuel-sipping crawl.
- Sizing conclusion, now the design rule: **the world was too big, not the
  rocket too small.** Circumference is the pacing lever. Shrinking it to the
  16–26 km band makes a leg 2.6–12 km, makes a 250–400 m pad 15–30 px wide on
  screen, and makes a hop a 20–30 s real-time event instead of a chore. Fuel,
  thrust and prices then follow from the shape of the leg.
- Second conclusion: one rocket cannot be the reference build for four
  gravities. Each planet preset now ships `defaultFuel`/`defaultPayload`, and
  the builder loads them into the config and the sliders on the first refresh
  and on every planet switch.

## Implemented
- `js/planets.js`: four presets (Verdant, Tinmoon, Cinder, Gossamer) carrying
  circumference, gravity, atmosphere, altitude map, landing tolerances, named
  pads, per-world prices, the reference build and optional autopilot tuning.
  `js/world.js` copies a preset into the live `world.planet` and rebuilds the
  pads, so gameplay modules never read a constants table.
- `js/autopilot.js`: command-layer guidance (velocity profile, shared thrust
  budget, brake feed-forward, staging on dry tanks, landing projection).
- `js/menu.js` + a builder header button: boot planet menu and mid-session
  planet switch, both starting a fresh run on the chosen world.
- Time control: a `[0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4]` ladder on `[`/`]`, HUD
  readout, default `2x`.
- Physics/economy/camera/mission generalized to the active planet; panel and
  HUD copy follows the chosen world.

## Bug hunt (each one reproduced headlessly before the fix)
- **Isp multiplied by local gravity.** Mass flow used `planet.surfaceGravity`,
  so on Tinmoon (g = 1.62) engines burned ~6× too slowly: every route ended in
  a mid-air fuel exhaustion and a crash at 85–152 s. Exhaust velocity is
  `Isp * 9.81` always.
- **Unbounded climb rate.** `sqrt(2*g*dh)` ignored what the descent profile
  could cancel; on Tinmoon the rocket topped out at 16.7 km and timed out
  after 20 minutes of simulated time. A `climbMargin` below 1 bounds it.
- **Phase ordering.** The old `if (wy < liftAltitude) phase = LIFT` kept
  boosting vertically over the pad, so the rocket left with `dx ≈ 0`. Cruise
  altitude is now fixed in the pad frame and LIFT only holds until the rocket
  is clear of the pad.
- **Airless Isp interpolation.** With `rho0 = 0` the sea/vacuum interpolation
  divided by zero, which resolved the Isp bug into a *slower* burn instead of
  vacuum Isp. Airless worlds use vacuum Isp directly.
- **Fuel-left telemetry read 0.0 kg** after accounting moved to per-stage
  masses; the envelope now sums the live stage masses.
- **Migration fallout:** the four suites still assumed `constants.world` and
  the old pad ids, and the render smoke test never loaded `menu.js`. Suites
  updated (builder test now also asserts the planet-switch defaults).

## Next step
Start 0.4 from the measured margins: the reference builds land with half their
fuel aboard and a `$60/kg` reward is a flat ~2× multiple over fuel cost, which
means the economy currently cannot tell a well-flown run from a wasteful one.
Turn the envelope numbers into a cost model with per-pad prices and a distance
bonus, then re-run the envelope as a balance experiment instead of a smoke test.
