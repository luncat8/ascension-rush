# Worklog — 0.5.2 Competitor pressure

## Starting point

0.5.1's damage and failure systems were implemented and measured. The remaining
0.5.2 specification called for a rival on the shared contract board, a real but
bounded flight, volume-based delivery pricing and a standings view. Review also
found one documentation ambiguity in 0.5.1: rated flights still accumulate a
small amount of cheap fatigue; it is sustained overload that wears stages much
faster. The constants comments now state that distinction, and the rupture
regression checks for the visible blast as well as mission adjudication.

## Plan review

The plan now specifies one rival courier and one opportunity after each
finished player flight leg, not a second player-facing flight or a second
economy. An out-and-back order pauses on the deck between its flights for the
player to authorize the return, so the rival can safely act at each landing.
Skybolt starts with
a catalog-built courier (the same starter-capital convention as the player),
its own $40,000, reputation and seeded skill. It can take only open postings at
its current pad. It shares the real contract board, destination prices and
market turn, while its fleet, cash, mission, guidance scratch state and random
stream are isolated. A lost ship must be rebuilt with rival funds. There is no
standing-service bot, campaign victory condition or per-frame rival work in
0.5.2.

The market needed a stronger immediate signal than the existing 1% delivery
nudge: the first 20-turn run hid rival activity in ordinary price drift. Raising
that nudge alone to 3% pushed established standing-service margins below their
0.4 balance band. The chosen rule is volume-scaled 3% pressure per reference
load, paired with 30% delivery-price reversion each market turn (fuel and steel
keep their existing 10% reversion). This makes busy-pad competition visible
without changing the existing steady-state service margin target.

## Implemented

- **`js/competitor.js`** owns Skybolt's type, fleet, cash, reputation, skill,
  bid stream and last-flight summary. It evaluates ready positive-profit
  contracts after refuelling, repair, overhaul, fee and expected cargo loss,
  then takes one deterministic opportunity at a bounded 20–60% probability.
  Profitable bids are never admitted through a non-finite estimate. A delivery
  increases skill and reputation; a failed flight reduces reputation and the
  ordinary mission rules determine vehicle and cargo losses.
- **`js/mission.js` / `js/operations.js`** trigger the rival after each
  finished player flight leg returns to the deck; an out-and-back order pauses
  there between flights for return authorization. The courier uses normal
  dispatch, autopilot, controls,
  fixed-step physics, damage, mission adjudication, economy and contract
  settlement. A 300-second simulated-time cap settles a guidance timeout through
  the ordinary failure path. Rival settlement does not advance the market a
  second time, append to the player's flight log or move the player's fleet,
  cash or autopilot state.
- **`js/market.js`** attributes delivered loads by operator and pad, records
  actual surviving cargo mass for contracts and standing service, and applies
  proportionate pressure to destination delivery prices. Empty cargo exerts no
  pressure. Prices remain within the existing market bounds and recover toward
  baseline. Player and rival delivery counts and kilograms are available to the
  Market view.
- **`js/deck.js`, `index.html`, `css/style.css`** show cash, deliveries and
  reputation side by side, and label rival-served volume on each pad's market
  card. The competitor script is loaded after operations and before the deck.
- **Documentation and tuning records** update `README.md`, `0.5-plan.md` and
  `AGENTS.md`; `experiments/competitor-test.js` covers seeded bidding, shared
  ownership, real flight resolution, payout, volume attribution, player-state
  isolation and paid rebuilding. The sweep clears route/result cells on passed
  turns rather than displaying a stale previous flight.

## Tuning result

`node experiments/competitor-sweep.js 20 20261006` records 20 Verdant market
turns. Skybolt accepted 6/20 eligible bids (30%), delivered all six, ended at
$50,936 from $40,000, reached reputation 56 and skill 0.40, and had no failure
in this short sample. The three served destinations recorded 210–240 kg each; their final delivery
prices were $16.80–$17.24/kg from opening quotes of $17.73–$18.49. Homeport
received no rival cargo and moved from $18.06 to $17.97/kg, near its $18
baseline. The complete row-by-row run
is `experiments/logs/0.5.2-competitor.txt`.

The market-pressure check also verifies that a reference load produces one
full pressure step, half a load produces half a step, and both companies' loads
are attributed independently. `experiments/logs/0.5.2-balance-pressure.txt`
records the ordinary balance run with volume pressure enabled. Its steady-state
reference-build margins remain in band: Verdant +11.7%, Tinmoon +14.7%, Cinder
+11.7%, and Gossamer +14.7% (target +10–20%). Re-running with the 0.5.1
damage model enabled also passes at +10.4 / +12.6 / +10.9 / +13.9%; both outputs
are retained as `0.5.2-balance-pressure*.txt`.

## Validation

- The seeded competitor integration test proves that a rival contract is
  resolved by more than 1,000 fixed physics steps and leaves exactly one
  player flight-log entry. It checks that a funded replacement is charged to
  Skybolt, not the player, and that an external timeout changes neither the
  player's log or reputation nor the shared market turn.
- The Market-tab render smoke test checks that the scoreboard names Skybolt,
  shows its reputation and renders rival service alongside the shared contract
  board.
- The damage suite asserts that a ruptured flight ends at the failure position
  with a visible blast, preserving the reviewed 0.5.1 failure presentation.
- All 13 `experiments/*-test.js` suites pass, including the competitor
  ownership/flight and Market-tab scoreboard checks. `node --check` passes for
  every JavaScript file under `js/` and `experiments/`, and `git diff --check`
  reports no whitespace errors.
- The seeded competitor sweep reproduces its saved log byte-for-byte. The
  balance-pressure run also reproduces its saved log byte-for-byte and keeps
  every established steady-state band in range.
- The browser smoke test runs against the real `index.html`; the served preview
  returns HTTP 200 with the competitor script and standings markup present.

## Result

0.5.2 ships as one deterministic rival turn per finished player-leg boundary,
sharing real contracts and destination prices while keeping the
competitor simulation bounded and outside the rendered frame loop. The exact
tuning and balance
outputs are preserved in `experiments/logs/`.
