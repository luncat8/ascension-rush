# Worklog 0.3.10 — The cruise line is the hold speed, and down is a choice

## Starting point

0.3.2 gave the guidance a cruise-speed guard — `vxDesired` capped at
`circularSpeedFraction` (0.7) × circular speed — and floored the vertical channel
at zero: `clamp(requestY, 0, budget)`, so the engine could push up and along but
**never down**. 0.3.7 then derived `v_circular` and `v_escape` properly from the
world's own `mu`. The two halves disagreed about what a hop is.

Measured before this change, on the shipped reference build:

- `experiments/envelope.js` delivered **44 of 48** directed routes. Tinmoon
  managed 8/12 balanced, 10/12 gentle — the long hops ended in a 300 s timeout,
  and one no-limits probe escaped to 206 km.
- The cap is a function of **altitude**. A flight above circular speed needs a
  downward push to keep up with the ground; the floor refused it, so the flight
  climbed, which raised the cap, which asked for more speed. The guard was a
  climb command.
- The economy records still looked fine (`0.5.2-balance-pressure.txt`, every band
  +11.7…+14.7%) because they were recorded against an older law. Flown against
  the shipped constants, the same report reads +23.8…+47.4% with three of four
  near-hop bands *positive* — a crawl so cheap that flooding a market pays.

All 20 test files pass at the end of this slice; they did not at its start (the
autopilot, campaign and trajectory files were red, and the campaign one was red in
a way that hid everything after its first stale assertion).

## What changed

**`physics.holdSpeedAtAltitude(altitude, downward)` (`js/physics.js`).** The speed
at which the ground curves away as fast as `downward` can follow it —
`sqrt((g(h) + max(0, downward)) * r)`. With `downward` zero it *is* circular speed,
so the new term is the whole difference: the boundary between a line gravity can
hold and one only thrust can is a boundary of the **engine**, not of the world.

**The cap now reads that figure with the budget in it** (`js/autopilot.js`):
`tuning.holdSpeedFraction * holdSpeedAtAltitude(rocket.wy, budget)`, and
`vectorY` is clamped to `±budget` instead of `[0, budget]`. The acceleration
limiter's flag follows the same symmetry (`Math.abs(requestY) > budget`). One
constant moved with it: `circularSpeedFraction: 0.7` → `holdSpeedFraction: 0.7`.

**Why 0.7 and not 1.** At the hold speed the vertical channel has spent the entire
budget, so the fraction is how much authority is left for the pad. It also decides
what a leg costs: at 1.0 a Cinder hop flies fast enough to burn its booster dry,
so the stage separates on **every** leg and the turnaround rebuilds it — 12 rebuilds
for 12 legs, $38 893 of structure against the old law's one, and the chain stops
broke at leg 18. At 0.7 the rebuild comes once in 12 legs exactly as it did under
the old law, and the same hop still lands 23% sooner. That was found in the ledger,
not in the margin column: the *band* was the symptom (+6.0% steady), the staging
cadence was the cause.

**What was measured and rejected.** Two levers were swept before the fraction was
chosen, both against `balance.js`'s bands with a `$TUNE` prelude (see below):
raising the cruise altitude (0.08→0.30 of the radius, 1200→3500 m) moves Verdant
from +6.0% to −38.7…−43.0% and Gossamer to −23.6…−27.3% — a higher line is a
longer climb on every hop, and the *low* cruise is the best of that family; cutting
`prices.fuel` (4.1→3.1, 2.6→1.2) pushes the steady margins to +26…+51% and turns
all four "the near hop must not pay" bands positive. Neither was kept: no price,
loadout or payload in `js/planets.js` moved for this slice.

**Tests rewritten where the law, not the code, was wrong.**
- `autopilot-test.js`'s Q-fade probe flew `{ wx: 1500, vy: 0 }` at 310-350 m/s and
  expected `(1 − band)` × the free thrust. Under the new cap the guidance *brakes*
  at those speeds, and braking is deliberately never faded, so the assertion was
  probing the exemption rather than the fade. It now sits on the free-fall line —
  circular speed at the cruise altitude, where the vertical channel is quiet and
  the whole request is along the airflow — with the world's pressure ceiling
  lowered for the probe so the band lands where a legal cruise actually flies, and
  the braking case moved to a speed inside the band. Two dead ends: with the probe
  at `wx: 0` on a 20 km planet a 10 km route is *equally* short backwards, so the
  guidance reverses and nothing about the fade is observable; and a first ratio set
  of 0.9 put the state *below* the fade band.
- The long-hop claim "the limits trim the peak pressure" is false on Verdant and
  Cinder now, and correctly so: the cruise law flies under the fade band
  (15.0 kPa against a 21 kPa fade start), so the limiter contributes nothing. The
  test asserts that instead — `limiterSeconds.Q === 0` where the free flight never
  reaches the band, and the trim where it does (Gossamer, 59.6 → 48.7 kPa).
- The frame-rate bound went from `|Δt| < 2 s` absolute to `steady.seconds * 0.06 +
  0.5`: `physics.advance` sub-steps at the fixed rate whatever the frame does, so
  all a frame rate changes is how often the guidance is re-asked — 7.5 Hz at
  30 fps × 4 — and a flight held by a capped line pays a few percent for a stale
  command (Cinder 88.96 s → 92.66 s).
- `trajectory-test.js`'s "every airborne step of a normal flight has a coast
  impact" is kept — it holds on all three atmospheric worlds now — but it runs on
  each of them, because at 1.0 it did not hold on any. Raising
  `trajectory.coastHorizon` was measured and rejected: 12-60% of airborne steps
  are genuinely unbound at that law, an invalid call costs 2.5 ms (14 400 steps)
  against 0.5 ms, and the true longest bound coast is 375 s.
- `experiments/balance.js` records a leg the harness stopped in mid-air as
  `STILL IN THE AIR` and ends the chain, instead of reading `entries[0]` of a
  flight that wrote no log entry and throwing.
- `market/operations/service-test.js` needed `require('../js/sky.js')`:
  `physics.atmosphereTop` reads `constants.sky`, which `physics.js` does not load
  itself, and the node harnesses list modules by hand.

## Verified

`experiments/logs/0.3.10-cruise.txt` — the 12 directed routes × 4 worlds, all three
profiles, every one delivered (was 44/48 on balanced). Mean hop time per world:
Verdant 91.8 s → 62.1 s, Cinder 89.7 s → 66.1 s, Gossamer 134.6 s → 76.0 s, and
Tinmoon's four timeouts are gone (its crawl mean was 192.2 s).

`experiments/logs/0.3.10-balance.txt` — every band passes with the shipped prices:
reference build +15.5% (target +10…+20), steady +12.0 / +17.3 / +14.6 / +10.1,
overbuilt near hop −19.1 / −0.2 / −19.0 / −14.8% (all ≤ 0), contracts +24.1% against
standing +9.3%, leftover fuel +90.0% against a refuelled +14.4%. The damage-on
repeat (`0.3.10-balance-damage.txt`) holds everywhere except Gossamer at +9.3%,
0.7 pp under the floor — recorded, not chased.

`experiments/logs/0.3.10-failures.txt` — a leg is shorter, so the stress it books on
its stages is lower (Verdant fresh 0.020/$17 → 0.016/$14) and the fragile rows read
better (fresh Verdant 0.50: 20.0% → 10.8% fail; worn Tinmoon 0.50: 74.2% → 38.3%).
The one row that moved the wrong way is the ordinary worn one: worn Verdant 1.00
6.7% → 10.8%, which is where the other worlds already sit, so `damage.failureRate`
stays where 0.5.1 tuned it.

The two campaign logs are regenerated and byte-compared by `campaign-test.js`. The
reference player settles Verdant's ladder on legs 1, 5, 9, 15, 32, 32 (the record
said 1, 5, 9, 15, 17, 19) and completes all four worlds within 37 legs each. 18 of
its 132 legs lose a few dollars apiece, all of them standing Tinmoon hops into a
saturated market — the price pressure doing its job against a fleet that now flies
2.4× the legs per world-hour.

`experiments/competitor-sweep.js 20 20261006` still reads like its record: 5 of 17
eligible bids taken (6 of 20 recorded), all delivered, prices drifting down per pad.

## Not verified

Nothing was flown in a browser. `index.html`, `js/render.js` and the CSS are
untouched, so the risk is in what the HUD now prints along the new line rather than
in what it draws: the coast row and the `UNBOUND` mark are read on a flight whose
cruise sits *just* under local escape speed (Verdant 216 m/s against 223 m/s at its
cruise altitude), which is a narrower margin than the plan's records assumed.

The campaign's pacing is reported, not settled. Its reference player flies 32 and 37
legs to clear the same two worlds it used to clear in 19 and 20 because a leg's cash
margin is thinner at the volumes the faster fleet creates; the lever for that is the
market's delivered-volume coefficient, and this slice deliberately did not touch it
with a margin band passing.

`holdSpeedFraction` is one number for four worlds. It happens to keep a flight bound
where gravity is worth something (0.49 × (g + budget) < 2g on Verdant) and does not
on Tinmoon (1.41 m/s² of gravity against 17 m/s² of engine, where the same
inequality is off by 3×). That is the honest reading of an airless rock — the coast
*does* go nowhere — but it means the fraction is doing two jobs: leaving arrival
authority, and keeping the delivery flights on the world.
