# Plan 0.2 — Rocket Physics, Stage Builder & Flight Controls

Subject scope: build a small staged rocket, launch from a pad, steer it with
mouse-directed thrust, and attempt a manual pad-to-pad flight. This slice adds
a first-pass economy and binary touchdown result; autopilot, planet selection,
parts persistence, and detailed damage are explicitly deferred to 0.3–0.5.

## Design decisions

- Keep the 0.1 world contract: `wx` is unbounded meters, `wy` is altitude in
  meters, pads are canonical longitudes, the camera follows horizontally, and
  only rendering uses the logarithmic altitude projection.
- Use a fixed 120 Hz semi-implicit Euler step with an accumulator. RAF time is
  capped at 1/30 s; landing is detected at the ground crossing and interpolated
  within the final physics step. `world.maxAltitude` is a display range, not a
  physics clamp.
- Gravity points down in the local flat-world frame and falls off as
  `g0 * (R / (R + max(wy, 0)))^2`. Drag is isotropic, quadratic, and based on
  the rocket's velocity through an exponential atmosphere. This simple model
  has no lift or attitude-dependent drag.
- The mouse aims the displayed nose in screen space; the same heading drives
  the thrust vector (`x = sin(heading)`, `y = cos(heading)`). This avoids
  asking players to reason through the log-Y inverse transform. Heading turns
  at a bounded rate; no arbitrary velocity-relative gimbal limit is applied.
- Stage 0 is the bottom booster and burns first. Space drops the active stage
  (including any leftover fuel) and immediately selects the next stage. An
  empty stage stays attached until Space is pressed. Throttle is held with
  Shift/Ctrl; Launch starts at full throttle.
- The 0.1 horizontal map compresses a full circumference into one viewport,
  so a 50 m landing radius would be sub-pixel. The world was therefore shrunk
  to a 16–26 km circumference, where a 250–400 m landing radius is 15–30 px,
  and touchdown keeps explicit vertical/horizontal speed limits and a HUD
  distance readout instead of relying on pixel precision.
- The builder is a DOM overlay; flight telemetry stays on canvas. Hull cost
  is charged once at launch, fuel is charged as consumed, and payload reward
  is paid only after a safe landing at the selected target. The 0.2 ledger is
  flight-aggregated; 0.4 replaces the prototype charge with persistent parts.

## Rocket model & balance

Each configured stage stores:

```
{
  fuelMass, fuelMax, dryMass, thrustMax, ispSea, ispVac,
  strength, alive
}
```

The rocket stores position/velocity, `heading` (radians; 0 up, positive east),
`stages` (bottom first), `stageCount`, `currentStage`, `throttle`, `payloadMass`,
`onGround`, `padId`, and flight flags. Dry mass is tank mass plus engine mass;
tank mass scales with capacity and strength, while engine mass scales with
rated thrust. Strength affects mass/cost and displays a future-failure warning;
there are no 0.2 random failures.

At each physics substep:

- `mass = payload + sum(dryMass + remaining fuel for alive stages)`.
- `thrust = thrustMax * throttle` for the active fueled stage.
- Interpolate Isp from sea-level to vacuum according to local density. Fuel
  flow is `thrust / (Isp * g0)` and is clamped to remaining fuel.
- Apply thrust, gravity and drag, then update velocity and position. Drag is
  `-0.5 * rho * Cd * A * |v| * v / mass`.
- Keep `wx` unbounded; use positive modulo / `wrapDelta` only for pad lookup.

The builder estimates staged dV with the Tsiolkovsky equation and removes a
stage's dry mass only after accounting for that stage's burn. It also reports
takeoff mass, steel, fuel, build cost and first-stage TWR. Payload capacity is
limited by a minimum launch TWR; the estimate is an idealized design readout,
not a promise of actual range (gravity and drag losses depend on the flown
trajectory).

Default tuning uses a three-stage stack so the last stage can be reserved for
approach and landing; it must lift off and complete the standard one-quarter
circumference route with fuel left over. From `constants.js`: exhaust velocity
`Isp * 9.81` (standard gravity, never local gravity), thrust `72 N` per kg of
fuel capacity, tank dry mass `7.5 %` of fuel capacity times the strength
factor, engine dry mass `60 kg + thrust / 150 N/kg`, sea/vacuum Isp `265/330 s`,
drag coefficient `0.22` on a `1.6 m²` reference area. Prices start at `$1.40/kg`
fuel, `$2.50/kg` dry structure and `$60/kg` delivered payload, with `$30,000`
starting cash. Circumference, gravity, atmosphere, pad layout, prices and the
reference build are per-planet (`archive/0.3.0-plan.md`); the sizes are the balance lever,
so a world is tuned by moving its pads closer, not by inflating its rockets.

Tune the values and measure with `node experiments/envelope.js <planet>` rather
than claiming a fixed dV-to-range conversion. The world's one-screen-per-planet
scale and multi-gravity band are deliberate arcade abstractions, not a
physically self-consistent planetary system.

## Time control
- The player can slow down or speed up the simulation over a `[0.5, 0.75, 1,
  1.5, 2, 3, 4]` ladder with `[` and `]`, shown in the flight HUD. The default
  is `2x`, because a standard hop is 40–60 s of simulated time and should read
  as 20–30 s of real time; `0.5x` is the landing-precision setting and `4x`
  shortens a long cruise.
- Time scale multiplies the simulation *budget* per frame, never the fixed
  integration step: `physics.advance` adds `frameDt * timeScale` to an
  accumulator and drains it in `1/120 s` steps, capped by `maxSubsteps`, so a
  flight is deterministic and reproduces at any multiplier. Manual turn and
  throttle rates are integrated with simulated time for the same reason.

## Builder & flight controls

- Show the builder only while the rocket is on a pad. It provides a target
  selector, one-to-three stages, per-stage fuel capacity and strength sliders,
  payload mass, estimated dV/mass/cost/TWR, affordability and lift-off warnings.
- Launch validates a different target, sufficient cash, and minimum TWR;
  commits the selected build and begins at full throttle. Editing is locked
  during flight.
- While flying, the mouse aims the nose/thrust vector. Shift increases and
  Ctrl decreases throttle continuously; Space stages once per key press.
  Controls are ignored in the builder and after touchdown.
- The flight HUD reports altitude, horizontal/vertical velocity, heading,
  target-pad distance, active stage/fuel, throttle, and cash balance. A small
  throttle flame makes engine response visible.

## Mission and economy

- On launch, charge the built dry mass at the steel price and record the
  departure/target pads. Fuel is deducted from cash as it burns; keep a single
  aggregate fuel ledger entry per flight, not one allocation per physics step.
- At ground contact, find the nearest pad by shortest wrap-aware distance.
  A landing is safe only within the pad radius and below both vertical and
  horizontal speed limits. Safe arrival at the selected pad pays
  `payloadMass * deliveryPricePerKg`; safe arrival at another pad pays no
  reward. All other contacts crash and lose the payload.
- A safe landing returns the player to the builder at the landing pad. A crash
  returns them to the departure pad with the flight report and costs retained.
  The binary crash/repair model is intentionally temporary; partial damage
  belongs to 0.5.

## Acceptance criteria

- A cold start at Homeport presents a responsive builder; target, stage count,
  each stage's fuel/strength, and payload are editable. Invalid TWR, an
  unchanged target, or an unaffordable build cannot launch.
- Launch starts the default three-stage build at full throttle. Mouse aim
  visibly turns the rocket, Shift/Ctrl alter throttle, and Space separates
  stages; an empty stage produces no thrust and can be discarded.
- Gravity and drag change the trajectory; fuel never goes negative; `wx`
  stays unbounded across the wrap seam; a fixed-step run is stable across RAF
  frame rates.
- Ground crossing resolves once: a safe target-pad touchdown pays the
  delivery reward, a safe off-target pad landing pays none, and an unsafe or
  off-pad impact crashes. The next build starts at the correct pad.
- HUD and builder show live flight/build values, target distance, costs and
  result. Cash changes equal hull charges minus consumed fuel plus earned
  reward.
- Node regression tests cover coordinate preservation, mass/staging/fuel,
  gravity/drag, touchdown outcomes, and app startup. dV is checked against its
  ideal rocket-equation calculation; it is not compared to full-flight dV
  including gravity/drag loss.
